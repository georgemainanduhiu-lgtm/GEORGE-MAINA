# Chapter 11: Kenan Yıldız, Believe in Yourself When Others Don't

> **FACT FILE**
>
> **Full name:** Kenan Yıldız (pronounced *keh-NAHN yuhl-DUHZ*)
>
> **Born:** May 4, 2005, in Regensburg, Germany
>
> **Position:** Forward / left winger
>
> **Famous for:** Wearing Juventus's legendary number 10 shirt and helping Turkey reach their first World Cup in twenty-four years
>
> **Superpower:** Self-belief, even when the people around you have stopped believing

## A Star Name

In Turkish, the word *yıldız* means **"star."**

That's a pretty good last name for a soccer player, isn't it? But Kenan Yıldız's story isn't about being a star from day one. It's about what happens when a big club decides you're *not* special enough, and you have to decide for yourself whether they're right.

## Two Countries, One Dream

Kenan was born in Regensburg, a beautiful old city on the Danube River in the south of Germany. His mother is German, and his father is from Turkey. So Kenan grew up between two cultures. He spoke German at school and with friends, and he learned about Turkish food, music, and family traditions at home.

Lots of kids grow up like Kenan, with family roots in more than one country. Maybe you do, too. It can sometimes feel confusing, like you have to pick one side. But it can also be a gift: two languages, two sets of traditions, two places to call home, and twice as many people to cheer for you.

And, of course, he played soccer. Lots of it.

He started at a local club in Regensburg. He was quick, he loved to dribble, and he had a beautiful touch on the ball. When he was about seven years old, he was spotted by scouts from the most powerful club in Germany: **Bayern Munich**.

For a young player in Germany, joining Bayern's academy is like winning the lottery. Bayern have won more German league titles than any other club, by far. They have huge stadiums, amazing training facilities, and some of the best coaches in the world.

Kenan joined Bayern's youth teams and stayed there for about ten years. He worked his way up, year after year, through every age group. He was often one of the best players on the team, and sometimes the captain. By the time he was sixteen, he was a key player for Bayern's under-19 team, playing with and against players several years older.

His dream seemed to be right on track.

## "I Never Felt Their Trust"

But here's the thing about big academies: they are full of talented players, and only a tiny number ever make it into the first team.

As Kenan got closer to the age when he could turn professional, he started to feel that Bayern didn't really see a future for him. He felt like the club wasn't giving him the chance or the confidence he needed. In 2022, when his youth contract was ending, Bayern and Kenan couldn't agree on a new contract.

So, at seventeen, after a decade at the club, Kenan **left Bayern Munich**.

Later, he explained his decision simply. He said that in all his years at Bayern, he never felt the club truly trusted him.

Think about how hard that must have been. Bayern was the only big club he had ever known. Leaving meant stepping into the unknown. What if no other big club wanted him? What if Bayern had been right not to fully believe in him?

But Kenan believed in himself. And it turned out that other clubs believed in him, too.

## A New Home in Turin

When word got around that Kenan was available, some famous clubs became interested, including Barcelona and **Juventus**, the most successful club in Italian history.

Kenan chose Juventus. In the summer of 2022, he moved to Turin, a city in northern Italy surrounded by mountains.

Once again, everything was new. He had to learn Italian. He had to get used to a new style of soccer, which in Italy is often very tactical, with defenders who are experts at stopping attackers. He started out in Juventus's youth team and then their reserve team, called Juventus Next Gen.

He was patient. He listened to his coaches. He worked on his strength and his decision-making. And he kept believing that his chance would come.

## A Debut, and a Dazzling Goal

It came sooner than almost anyone expected. On the first day of the 2023–24 Serie A season, in August 2023, eighteen-year-old Kenan came off the bench to make his debut for Juventus's first team.

A few months later, on December 23, 2023, he got his first start in Serie A, away at a club called Frosinone. In the 12th minute, he got the ball near the edge of the penalty area. He twisted past one defender. Then another. Then a third. And then he drilled a shot into the net.

At eighteen years and 233 days old, Kenan became the youngest foreign player ever to score for Juventus in Serie A.

Juventus fans were thrilled. Some of them started comparing him to one of the greatest players in the club's history: Alessandro Del Piero, who was famous for curling shots from the left side of the penalty area into the far corner of the net. Kenan loved that kind of shot, too.

That season, Kenan also won his first trophy as a professional when Juventus won the Coppa Italia, Italy's national cup competition.

## Choosing Turkey

Because he was born in Germany and his father came from Turkey, Kenan could choose to play international soccer for either country. It was a big decision. He chose **Turkey**, the country of his father's birth.

In October 2023, he made his debut for Turkey's senior national team, coming on as a substitute in a win over Croatia. Just one month later, Turkey played a friendly against Germany in Berlin. And Kenan, playing against the country where he was born, scored his first goal for Turkey as they won 3–2.

In the summer of 2024, the European Championship was held in Germany, and Kenan was part of Turkey's squad. It was a special experience: playing for Turkey in the country where he grew up, in front of huge crowds of Turkish fans, many of whom also live in Germany. Turkey made it all the way to the quarterfinals.

## The Number 10

In the summer of 2024, Juventus gave Kenan something very special: the **number 10 shirt**.

At Juventus, the number 10 is sacred. It has been worn by some of the greatest players ever: Michel Platini, Roberto Baggio, and Alessandro Del Piero, the very player Kenan had been compared to. Giving it to a nineteen-year-old was a huge statement. It meant the club believed he could become one of its leaders.

Remember the boy who felt that Bayern Munich never really trusted him? Now one of the biggest clubs in the world was trusting him with its most famous shirt.

## The Road to the World Cup

Turkey had not played at a World Cup since 2002, when they finished third. That was before Kenan was even born. For twenty-four years, Turkish fans had been waiting.

In qualifying for the 2026 World Cup, Turkey were in a group with Spain, the European champions. In September 2025, Spain crushed Turkey **6–0** in front of Turkey's own fans. It was Turkey's worst defeat in a competitive game in sixty years.

It would have been easy for the team to fall apart after that. Instead, they regrouped. They finished second in their group and earned a spot in the playoffs.

In March 2026, Turkey beat Romania 1–0 in the playoff semifinal. Then, in the playoff final against Kosovo, in a tense, nervous game, Kenan dribbled down the left side early in the second half and cut the ball back into the penalty area. His teammate Orkun Kökçü got a shot away, and Kerem Aktürkoğlu pounced to score. **Turkey won 1–0.**

After twenty-four years, Turkey were going back to the World Cup!

## A Tough Lesson, and a Bright Future

The 2026 World Cup didn't go the way Kenan and his teammates had dreamed. Turkey lost their first two games, against Australia and Paraguay. In their final group game, they played the United States, one of the host countries, and won a thrilling match 3–2 with a goal in the very last moments. But it wasn't enough. Turkey were knocked out in the group stage, and Kenan didn't score.

It was a painful lesson. But Kenan was only twenty-one. He had already been through bigger disappointments than this, like leaving the only club he'd ever known. He knew how to come back.

Many people believe that Kenan Yıldız's best years are still ahead of him. If his story so far tells us anything, it's that he won't let one setback decide his future.

## Teamwork Spotlight: Turkey's Young Stars

Kenan isn't the only exciting young player on Turkey's team. He shares the spotlight with another rising star: **Arda Güler**, who plays for Real Madrid.

Arda is from Turkey's capital city, Ankara, and he's a left-footed playmaker with a magical touch, just like Kenan. Some people thought two young stars might get in each other's way. Instead, they've become a dangerous duo. When Turkey beat the United States 3–2 at the 2026 World Cup, it was Arda who scored Turkey's first goal.

Turkey's team also has experienced leaders who help the younger players grow, like their captain, the midfielder Hakan Çalhanoğlu. And their coach, Vincenzo Montella, is a former Italian striker who understands what it's like to be a young attacker in a big moment.

Kenan's assist in the playoff win over Kosovo shows exactly how teamwork works. He made the run and the cutback. A teammate took the shot. Another teammate scored. Three players, one goal, and a whole nation celebrating.

The best young players don't try to do everything by themselves. They learn from the older players, connect with the other young stars, and trust their teammates to finish what they start.

## Never-Give-Up Lesson

**Other people's doubts don't have to become your own.**

Kenan spent ten years at Bayern Munich and never felt the club truly believed in him. He could have decided they were right. Instead, he trusted himself, took a brave step into the unknown, and found a club that gave him their most famous shirt. Sometimes the people around you won't see what you can become. That's when your own belief matters most.

## Fun Facts

- Kenan's last name, *Yıldız*, means "star" in Turkish.
- At Juventus, he wears number 10, the same number worn by legends Platini, Baggio, and Del Piero.
- His first goal for Turkey came against Germany, the country where he was born.
- In Turkish, the letter "ı" (an "i" without a dot) makes a different sound from the normal "i." That's why his name is written *Yıldız*.
- Turkey's 2026 World Cup was their first in twenty-four years, and only their third ever.
