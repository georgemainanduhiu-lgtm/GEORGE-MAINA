# Chapter 3: Luka Modrić, From War Refugee to the Best in the World

> **FACT FILE**
>
> **Full name:** Luka Modrić
>
> **Born:** September 9, 1985, in Zadar, Croatia
>
> **Position:** Central midfielder
>
> **Famous for:** Winning the 2018 Ballon d'Or, six Champions League titles with Real Madrid, and leading tiny Croatia to a World Cup final
>
> **Superpower:** Staying calm and brave, even when the world around him is falling apart

## The Goat Boy of the Mountains

High up in the rocky hills of Croatia, at the foot of the Velebit mountains, there was a tiny cluster of stone houses called Modrići. It was named after the family that lived there: the Modrić family.

In one of those stone houses, a little boy named Luka spent much of his early childhood with his grandfather, who was also named Luka. His parents worked long hours in the nearby town, so Grandpa Luka looked after him. The two of them were best friends. Little Luka followed his grandfather everywhere, including up the hillsides to look after the family's goats.

There were no fancy soccer fields in the mountains. But Luka had a ball, and he kicked it everywhere: against the stone walls, along the dirt paths, between the rocks. Life was simple and peaceful.

Then, when Luka was six years old, everything changed.

## The Day the War Came

In 1991, Croatia declared that it wanted to be its own independent country. Before that, it had been part of a larger country called Yugoslavia. Not everyone agreed, and a terrible war began.

In December 1991, soldiers came to the area where the Modrić family lived. Luka's beloved grandfather was out with the animals, and he was killed. Luka's family had to run for their lives. Their house was burned to the ground.

They escaped to the nearby city of Zadar. But they had no home anymore. They became **refugees**, people who have been forced to leave their homes because of war or danger.

The family moved into a hotel called the Kolovare, along with many other families who had lost their homes. It wasn't a vacation. It was crowded, and the family had very little money. They lived in hotels for years.

And the war wasn't over. Zadar was attacked too. Bombs and shells fell on the city. When the warning sirens howled, Luka and the other kids had to run down to shelters and wait until it was safe to come out again.

This was Luka Modrić's childhood. It's hard to imagine, isn't it?

## A Ball in the Parking Lot

But here's what's amazing. In the middle of all that fear and sadness, Luka still played.

He played soccer in the hotel's parking lot every single day. He kicked the ball against the hotel walls. He broke more than a few windows! He dribbled around parked cars. The other children played with him, and for a few hours, they could forget about the war.

Later, Luka said that during those years, soccer was what kept him going. The ball was his escape. It was the one thing that made him feel like a normal kid.

He wore shin guards with a picture of his hero, Ronaldo (the Brazilian striker, not Cristiano), on them. He dreamed of playing on big stages. And people around Zadar started noticing that the little refugee boy from the hotel was very, very good.

## "Too Small, Too Weak"

Luka joined the youth teams of NK Zadar, the local club. A coach there named Tomislav Bašić saw something special in him. He helped Luka's family, encouraged him, and became like a second father to him.

When Luka was about ten, he got a big chance: a trial with Hajduk Split, one of the most famous clubs in Croatia and the team he supported.

He traveled to Split with high hopes. He played his heart out.

And they said no.

Hajduk Split told him he was **too small and too weak** to become a professional player.

Luka was crushed. He was a skinny kid, it was true. He was shorter than the other boys. But he could see the whole field. He could pass the ball exactly where it needed to go. Didn't that count for anything?

He went home to Zadar, and he made a choice. He wasn't going to grow taller just by wishing. So instead, he would become so clever, so skillful, and so tough that his size wouldn't matter.

## Toughened Up in Bosnia

When Luka was sixteen, a bigger club came calling: Dinamo Zagreb, the most successful team in Croatia. They signed him in 2002.

But Dinamo didn't put him straight into their first team. Instead, they sent him on **loan** (which means playing for another club for a while) to a team called Zrinjski Mostar, in the country next door, Bosnia and Herzegovina.

The league in Bosnia was famous for being rough. The fields were bumpy. The defenders were big, strong men who tackled hard. For a skinny eighteen-year-old midfielder, it could have been a disaster.

Instead, Luka loved the challenge. He learned to protect the ball, ride tackles, and get up every time he was knocked down. At the end of the season, he was voted the best player in the whole league. Luka later said that if you can play in the Bosnian league, you can play anywhere.

He went on another loan, to a Croatian club called Inter Zaprešić, and helped them finish second in the league. Then he returned to Dinamo Zagreb and became their star, winning three league titles in a row.

The boy who had been "too small" for Hajduk was now the best player in Croatia.

## Worst Signing of the Year

In 2008, Luka moved to England to play for Tottenham Hotspur. Some people said the Premier League was too physical for a little guy like him. He proved them wrong, and soon he was one of the best midfielders in England.

In 2012, the biggest club in the world came knocking: Real Madrid.

You might think that was the moment Luka finally made it. But his first few months in Madrid were tough. He didn't get many chances to start. He was still adjusting. At the end of his first season, a popular Spanish sports newspaper asked its readers to vote for the **worst signing of the season** in La Liga.

And the winner was … Luka Modrić.

Can you imagine reading that about yourself? Thousands of people voting that you're the biggest flop in the league?

Luka didn't argue. He didn't complain. He did what he'd always done: he worked, he kept passing the ball beautifully, and he let his playing answer for him.

## The Corner That Changed History

In May 2014, Real Madrid played their city rivals, Atlético Madrid, in the Champions League final in Lisbon. Real Madrid hadn't won the Champions League in twelve years, and the fans were desperate.

Atlético were winning 1–0. The clock ticked into the last minutes. Then, in the 93rd minute, Real Madrid got a corner kick.

Luka Modrić walked over to take it. He curled the ball into the crowded penalty area, and the defender Sergio Ramos leaped up and headed it into the net. **1–1!** Real Madrid went on to win the match 4–1 in extra time.

That was the first of **six** Champions League titles Luka would win with Real Madrid. The "worst signing of the season" had become one of the greatest players in the history of the club. By the time he left Madrid in 2025, he had won more trophies than anyone else who had ever played for Real Madrid.

## The Little Country with the Giant Heart

Croatia is a small country, with only about four million people. That's fewer people than live in many big cities! For years, Croatia had good players but never seemed to go very far at big tournaments.

At the 2018 World Cup in Russia, Luka Modrić was Croatia's captain. And something magical happened.

In the round of 16, Croatia went to a penalty shootout against Denmark, and won. In the quarterfinal, they went to *another* penalty shootout against Russia, and won again. In the semifinal against England, they fell behind 1–0 early. Many teams would have given up. Croatia's players were exhausted from all those long games. But they kept running. They tied the game, and then won it 2–1 in extra time.

Three knockout games in a row that went to extra time, and Croatia found a way to win all three. They had a never-give-up attitude, and their little captain was the heart of it.

Croatia lost the final to France. But at the end of the tournament, Luka was given the Golden Ball as the best player of the whole World Cup. And a few months later, he won the **Ballon d'Or**.

For ten years, from 2008 to 2017, only two players had won the Ballon d'Or: Lionel Messi and Cristiano Ronaldo. Luka Modrić, the refugee from the Kolovare hotel, was the one who finally broke that streak.

## Still Running at Forty

Most midfielders slow down in their thirties. Luka just kept going.

At the 2022 World Cup, at age thirty-seven, he led Croatia to third place. In 2025, after thirteen seasons at Real Madrid, he said goodbye and joined AC Milan in Italy, turning forty a couple of months later. And in 2026, he played at his fifth World Cup, still wearing Croatia's captain's armband and still spraying passes around the field like a magician.

He is the most-capped player in Croatia's history, meaning he has played more games for his country than anyone else.

When people ask Luka how he made it, he often talks about his childhood. The war taught him that life can be hard and unfair. But it also taught him to be strong, to appreciate every moment, and to never give up, no matter what.

## Teamwork Spotlight: Croatia's Band of Brothers

Croatia's run to the 2018 World Cup final was a team effort from start to finish.

In the two penalty shootouts against Denmark and Russia, Croatia's goalkeeper, Danijel Subašić, was a hero. Against Denmark, he saved three penalties! In the midfield next to Luka, his friend Ivan Rakitić ran and passed for mile after mile. And in the semifinal against England, it was the tough striker Mario Mandžukić who scored the winning goal in extra time.

Even Luka had his low moments. In the round of 16 against Denmark, he had a penalty saved in extra time, a miss that could have knocked Croatia out. But his teammates picked him up. And when the game went to a shootout, Luka bravely stepped up again and scored.

That's what a team does. When one player stumbles, the others lift him. Then, next time, he lifts them.

Luka has always said that the most important thing about that Croatian team was how close the players were. They weren't just teammates. They were a family. And when a family of four million people's sons all pull in the same direction, they can reach a World Cup final.

## Never-Give-Up Lesson

**Hard times can make you stronger, if you let them.**

Luka Modrić lost his grandfather and his home when he was just six. He was told he was too small. He was voted the worst signing of the year. Any one of those things could have stopped him. Instead, each one made him tougher, smarter, and more determined.

Your past doesn't decide your future. *You* do.

## Fun Facts

- Luka's first big hero was the Brazilian striker Ronaldo, and as a boy he wore shin guards with Ronaldo's picture on them.
- His nickname in Croatia is sometimes "the Croatian Cruyff," after the Dutch legend Johan Cruyff.
- He is famous for the "trivela," a pass or shot struck with the outside of his foot that curves through the air.
- He won six Champions League titles with Real Madrid: in 2014, 2016, 2017, 2018, 2022, and 2024.
- Croatia have reached two World Cup semifinals with Luka as captain: in 2018 (they finished second) and 2022 (third).
