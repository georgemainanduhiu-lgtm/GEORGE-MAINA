# Chapter 8: Erling Haaland, The Late Bloomer from Bryne

> **FACT FILE**
>
> **Full name:** Erling Braut Haaland
>
> **Born:** July 21, 2000, in Leeds, England (but he grew up in Norway)
>
> **Position:** Striker
>
> **Famous for:** Scoring 36 goals in a single Premier League season, winning the treble with Manchester City, and leading Norway to their best-ever World Cup
>
> **Superpower:** A hunger for goals that never runs out, and the patience to wait for his moment

## The Five-Year-Old World Record Holder

Here's a strange fact to start this chapter: when Erling Haaland was five years old, he set a world record.

Not in soccer. In the **standing long jump**.

The standing long jump is when you stand still, swing your arms, and leap forward as far as you can, without a running start. In 2006, little Erling jumped 1.63 meters (more than 5 feet 4 inches). That was reported to be a world record for a five-year-old!

That tells you two things about Erling. First, he had a lot of natural power in his legs. Second, he loved to compete, at *everything*.

## A Sporty Kid in a Small Town

Erling was born in Leeds, England, because his dad, Alf-Inge Haaland, was playing soccer there for Leeds United. His mom, Gry Marita Braut, was a top athlete too: she competed in the heptathlon, a track-and-field event made up of seven different sports.

When Erling was three, the family moved back to their home in Norway, to a small town called **Bryne**, on the country's windy southwest coast. Bryne has about 12,000 people. It's surrounded by farms, and in the winter it gets dark early and cold.

Erling tried lots of sports as a kid. He played handball. He did track and field. He went cross-country skiing. And of course, he played soccer, at Bryne FK, the local club.

## Not the Best Kid on the Team

Now here's the part of Erling's story that surprises most people.

When he was young, Erling was **not** the best player on his team.

He was small for his age for a while and a little clumsy. Some of the other boys were quicker, more skillful, or more mature. His youth coach, Alf Ingve Berntsen, has said that Erling wasn't the most talented player in his group when he was little. Nobody looked at young Erling and thought, "That kid is going to be one of the best strikers in the world."

But Berntsen noticed something else about him: Erling's **attitude**. He hated losing. He always wanted to improve. He listened. He trained hard. And he never, ever stopped trying to score.

At Bryne, the coaches believed every kid deserved a chance to develop, not just the ones who looked like stars at age ten. They kept Erling playing and learning. And then, as a teenager, Erling went through a giant growth spurt. He shot up taller than almost everyone. He kept his quick feet, but now he had size and strength too.

The late bloomer was starting to bloom.

## Sixteen Games, Zero Goals

In May 2016, when Erling was just fifteen years old, Bryne gave him his debut for the first team, playing against grown men in Norway's second division.

He had scored lots of goals for the reserve team. So you'd expect him to score straight away for the first team, right?

He didn't.

Erling played sixteen games for Bryne's first team. And in those sixteen games, he scored **zero goals**.

Not one.

Think about that. Today, Erling Haaland is famous for scoring goals faster than almost anyone in history. But at the start of his career, he went sixteen games without scoring a single one.

Did he quit? Did he decide he wasn't a striker after all? No. He kept working on his game. He got stronger. He learned from playing against tough, experienced defenders. He kept getting into the right positions, believing that the goals would come.

## Four Goals in Twenty-One Minutes

In 2017, a bigger Norwegian club called Molde signed him. Molde's coach was Ole Gunnar Solskjær, a famous former striker who had scored the winning goal for Manchester United in a Champions League final.

Solskjær helped Erling learn the art of being a striker: how to move in the penalty area, how to lose defenders, and how to finish calmly.

In July 2018, in a game against a team called Brann, Erling scored **four goals in twenty-one minutes**. The boy who couldn't score at Bryne was suddenly unstoppable.

## Nine Goals in One Game

In 2019, Erling played for Norway at the Under-20 World Cup in Poland. In a game against Honduras, he scored **nine goals**. Nine! Norway won 12–0, and Erling set a record for the most goals by a player in a single game at that tournament.

That same year, he moved to Red Bull Salzburg in Austria. In his very first Champions League game, he scored a hat-trick.

Then, in January 2020, he joined Borussia Dortmund in Germany. In his first game, he came on as a substitute in the second half, with his team losing. In about twenty minutes, he scored **three goals**. A hat-trick off the bench, on his debut!

People everywhere started asking: who is this guy?

## Breaking Records in England

In 2022, Erling moved to Manchester City in England, the country where he'd been born.

In his first season, he scored **36 goals in the Premier League**. That broke the record for the most goals in a single Premier League season. (The record for a 38-game season had belonged to Mohamed Salah, from Chapter 4, with 32.) Overall that season, he scored 52 goals in all competitions.

And Manchester City won the **treble**: the Premier League, the FA Cup, and the Champions League, all in the same season.

The last piece of that treble came in June 2023, in Istanbul, when City beat Inter Milan 1–0 in the Champions League final. Erling didn't score that night. Inter's defenders watched him like hawks. But by pulling defenders toward him, he helped create space for his teammates, and the midfielder Rodri scored the winning goal. Sometimes the best thing a striker can do is make room for someone else.

Erling also has some unusual habits that he believes help him play his best. He meditates to calm his mind. He wears special glasses in the evening that block blue light from screens, to help him sleep. He takes cold plunges in icy water. And he eats very healthy food, including things most kids would turn their noses up at, like heart and liver! His famous goal celebration, sitting cross-legged with his eyes closed and his hands resting on his knees, is a meditation pose.

## The Star Without a Tournament

But for years, there was one thing Erling didn't have: a big international tournament.

Norway is a small country, with about five and a half million people. The national team hadn't played at a World Cup since 1998, or at a European Championship since 2000. Erling scored lots of goals for Norway, but they kept falling short. They missed Euro 2020. They missed the 2022 World Cup. They missed Euro 2024.

Some people said it was a shame: one of the best strikers in the world might never get to play at a World Cup. Imagine being that good and never getting the chance!

Erling never stopped believing. And in the qualifying games for the 2026 World Cup, Norway were incredible. They won **all eight** of their games. They beat Italy, four-time world champions, twice, including a 4–1 win in Milan. And Erling scored **sixteen goals** in those eight games.

After almost thirty years, Norway were going back to the World Cup.

## Norway's Greatest Summer

In the summer of 2026, Erling finally walked out at a World Cup.

And he scored in **every one** of Norway's group games. In the last one, his two goals helped Norway beat Senegal 3–2 and move into the knockout rounds.

In the round of 32, against the Ivory Coast, Erling scored a late winner. It was Norway's first-ever win in a World Cup knockout game.

Then came the round of 16, against Brazil, the most successful World Cup team in history, with stars like Vinícius Júnior (Chapter 7). Erling scored twice, and Norway won 2–1. The whole of Norway went wild.

In the quarterfinal, Norway played England. It was a thrilling, exhausting game that went to extra time, and England's Jude Bellingham (Chapter 10) scored twice to win it 2–1. Norway's amazing run was over.

Erling was disappointed. But he had scored seven goals at the World Cup and led his small country to the quarterfinals for the first time ever. The boy who couldn't score in his first sixteen games for Bryne had become a national hero.

## Teamwork Spotlight: The Playmaker and the Finisher

Every great striker needs someone to pass them the ball. For Norway, that someone is often **Martin Ødegaard**.

Ødegaard was a child prodigy in Norway. He played for the national team when he was only fifteen and signed for Real Madrid as a teenager. Then he went through his own hard years, moving on loan from club to club, before he found a home at Arsenal and became their captain. (Yes, he's the same captain who lifted the Premier League trophy with Bukayo Saka in Chapter 9!)

For Norway, Ødegaard is the captain and the playmaker: the one who sees the pass. Haaland is the finisher: the one who puts it in the net. Together, they turned a team that kept missing tournaments into one that won all eight of its World Cup qualifiers.

At Manchester City, Erling also had help from brilliant passers, especially the Belgian midfielder Kevin De Bruyne, who set up many of his record-breaking 36 goals.

And here's a fun connection: when Erling played for Borussia Dortmund, one of his teammates was a teenager named Jude Bellingham. They became friends. Years later, at the 2026 World Cup, the two friends met again as opponents in a quarterfinal.

## Never-Give-Up Lesson

**It doesn't matter how fast you start. It matters that you keep going.**

Erling Haaland wasn't the best kid on his team. He went sixteen games without a goal in his first season as a pro. His country kept missing out on big tournaments. If he had judged himself only by those early results, he might have quit. Instead, he trusted the process, worked hard every day, and waited for his moment. When it came, he was ready.

If you're a "late bloomer" at something, that's okay. Some of the best things take time to grow.

## Fun Facts

- Erling's dad, Alf-Inge Haaland, also played in the Premier League, for Nottingham Forest, Leeds United, and Manchester City.
- His nine goals in one game at the 2019 Under-20 World Cup is a record for that tournament.
- In January 2025, he signed a contract with Manchester City that runs until 2034, one of the longest contracts in soccer history.
- He is about 1.95 meters (6 feet 5 inches) tall, yet he's also one of the fastest players in the Premier League.
- He has won the Premier League Golden Boot as the league's top scorer in his first two seasons in England.
