# Chapter 9: Bukayo Saka, The Penalty That Didn't Break Him

> **FACT FILE**
>
> **Full name:** Bukayo Ayoyinka Temidayo Saka
>
> **Born:** September 5, 2001, in London, England
>
> **Position:** Right winger
>
> **Famous for:** Coming up through Arsenal's academy to win the Premier League, being named England's Player of the Year twice, and bouncing back from a penalty miss in a European Championship final
>
> **Superpower:** A smile and a quiet strength that nobody can knock down

## A Name That Means Joy

In the Yoruba language, which is spoken by millions of people in Nigeria, the name **Bukayo** means "adds to joy." It's a perfect name for a boy who would grow up to bring joy to millions of soccer fans, and who would need every bit of his own inner joy to get through one of the hardest moments any young athlete has ever faced.

Bukayo's parents, Yomi and Adenike, moved to London from Nigeria. They worked hard, and they had big hopes for their children. They raised Bukayo in Ealing, in West London, and they taught him that being a good person mattered more than anything else.

Bukayo loved soccer. He played in the park, in the playground, and anywhere else he could. When he was seven years old, scouts from Arsenal, one of England's biggest clubs, saw him playing and invited him to join their youth academy, which is called **Hale End**.

He would stay at Arsenal for the rest of his career so far: through childhood, through his teenage years, and all the way to becoming one of the club's biggest stars.

## School Comes First

Bukayo's parents had one very important rule: **school comes first.**

Being at Arsenal's academy was exciting, but Bukayo's dad knew that only a small number of academy players ever become professionals. He wanted Bukayo to have a good education no matter what happened with soccer. So if Bukayo wanted to play, he needed to work hard in the classroom, too.

And he did. When he took his GCSE exams (the big tests that British students take at around age sixteen), Bukayo earned four A\*s and three As. Those are top grades!

Think about how hard that is: training with a professional soccer club several days a week *and* getting top marks at school. It took discipline, focus, and a lot of late nights with his schoolbooks. It also taught him something that would help him later: how to keep going when you're tired, and how to stay calm under pressure.

## A Debut at Seventeen

In November 2018, when he was just seventeen years old, Bukayo made his debut for Arsenal's first team in a Europa League game. A couple of months later, he played his first Premier League game.

His big breakthrough came during the 2019–20 season. Arsenal had lots of injured players, and the coach needed someone to play at **left back**, a defender's position. Bukayo was a winger who loved to attack, but he said yes. He played left back, and he was excellent!

Over the next season, Arsenal's manager Mikel Arteta moved him around: left back, left wing, right wing, midfield. Wherever he played, Bukayo worked hard and did what the team needed. He became known as one of the most selfless young players in England.

And by the summer of 2021, at nineteen years old, he was playing for England at the European Championship.

## The Longest Walk

England's run at Euro 2020 (played in 2021, because of the pandemic) was magical. The team reached the final for the first time ever, and the final was at Wembley Stadium in London, Bukayo's hometown.

On July 11, 2021, England played Italy in front of a huge home crowd. England scored early. Italy tied the game. After 120 minutes, it was still 1–1.

It was going to be decided by a penalty shootout.

Bukayo, the youngest player on the team, was chosen to take England's fifth penalty. When his turn came, England were behind. If he missed, Italy would win.

He walked from the halfway line to the penalty spot. It's a long walk, and every step must have felt like a mile. Tens of thousands of people in the stadium. Millions more watching on TV.

He struck the ball low. Italy's giant goalkeeper, Gianluigi Donnarumma, dived the right way and saved it.

Italy were champions of Europe. Bukayo stood with his hands on his face. His teammates and his manager, Gareth Southgate, rushed to hug him.

## When People Were Cruel

What happened next was very sad.

Bukayo, along with two of his teammates who had also missed penalties that night, Marcus Rashford and Jadon Sancho, received thousands of racist messages online. People insulted them because they were Black. It was ugly and shameful.

Bukayo was nineteen years old. He had just been brave enough to take a penalty in a European Championship final, and some people were treating him horribly for it.

But then something else happened, too.

Thousands and thousands of people sent Bukayo messages of **love and support**. Fans, players, kids, teachers, and even people who didn't usually care about soccer wrote to say they were proud of him. Many said they would have been too scared to take a penalty at all.

A few days later, Bukayo shared a message with the world. He thanked everyone who supported him. He spoke out against the racist abuse and said that social media companies needed to do more to stop it. And he made a promise to himself and to everyone watching. He wrote:

*"I will not let this moment or negativity break me."*

And he didn't.

## The Loudest Cheer

Less than two months later, on September 5, 2021, England played their first game back at Wembley Stadium since the final. They were playing Andorra in a World Cup qualifier.

It was also Bukayo's twentieth birthday.

Before the game, when his name was read out over the loudspeakers, the crowd gave him the **loudest cheer of the night**. They stood and clapped and sang his name. They wanted him to know they were on his side.

And then, late in the game, Bukayo jumped up and headed in a goal.

Wembley erupted. It was as if the whole stadium was giving him a giant hug. After the match, Bukayo said that playing at Wembley in front of his family and scoring on his birthday was what he dreamed about.

## Starboy

From then on, Bukayo just kept getting better.

He was voted **England's Men's Player of the Year** for the 2021–22 season, and then *again* for the 2022–23 season. At the 2022 World Cup in Qatar, he scored three goals, including two in England's very first game.

Then, at Euro 2024, came a moment that felt like it was written just for him. In the quarterfinal against Switzerland, England were losing 1–0 with ten minutes left. Bukayo got the ball on the right, cut inside, and curled a beautiful left-footed shot into the bottom corner. **1–1!**

The game went to... a penalty shootout. The same kind of shootout that had broken so many hearts three years before.

Bukayo stepped up to take England's third penalty. This time, he sent the goalkeeper the wrong way and scored. England won the shootout. Bukayo smiled the biggest smile you've ever seen. He had faced his biggest fear, and he had won.

Arsenal fans love him so much that they call him **"Starboy."**

## Champions at Last

In December 2024, Bukayo hurt his hamstring badly and needed an operation. He was out for over three months. When he finally came back in April 2025, he scored within minutes of stepping onto the field.

Arsenal had been close to winning the Premier League for several seasons, finishing second three years in a row. Lots of people said they'd never quite get over the line.

But in the 2025–26 season, Arsenal finally did it. In May 2026, they were crowned **Premier League champions** for the first time in twenty-two years. Bukayo, the boy who had joined Arsenal's academy at seven, lifted the trophy with the club he'd grown up with.

A few days later, Arsenal lost the Champions League final to Paris Saint-Germain on penalties (Ousmane Dembélé's team, from Chapter 6). It hurt. But they were champions of England, and Bukayo had helped make it happen.

That summer, he played at the 2026 World Cup, where England reached the semifinals and then beat France to finish **third**, England's best World Cup finish since they won it way back in 1966.

## Giving Back

In 2023, Bukayo paid for 120 children in Nigeria to have operations that they needed but couldn't afford. He didn't make a big fuss about it. He just wanted to help kids in the country his family comes from.

Remember what his name means? *Adds to joy.* He really does.

## Teamwork Spotlight: "That's on Me"

After England lost the Euro 2020 final on penalties, England's manager, Gareth Southgate, did something important.

He stood in front of the cameras and said that the decision about who took the penalties was **his** decision. He said the blame belonged to him, not to the young players who had been brave enough to step up. He wanted to protect Bukayo and his teammates.

Bukayo's Arsenal family protected him, too. His manager, Mikel Arteta, and his teammates surrounded him with support when he came back to the club. When he played his first games back, Arsenal fans sang his name louder than ever.

At Arsenal, Bukayo also found a perfect partner on the right side of the field. Captain Martin Ødegaard plays just inside him, and the two of them have an almost magical understanding: Ødegaard slips a pass, Bukayo makes the run. Behind them, the right back overlaps, and the whole team moves together.

Nobody can stop a hard moment from happening. But good teammates and good leaders can make sure you don't have to face it by yourself. That's what Bukayo's coaches and teammates did for him, and it's what he now does for the younger players coming up behind him.

## Never-Give-Up Lesson

**Your hardest moment doesn't get to decide who you are.**

Bukayo Saka missed a penalty in a European Championship final at nineteen, and then faced terrible abuse. He could have hidden from soccer, or from penalties, forever. Instead, he leaned on the people who loved him, kept being kind, and stepped up to the penalty spot again when it mattered. Being brave isn't about never being scared. It's about stepping up even when you are.

## Fun Facts

- Bukayo is left-footed but plays on the right wing, so he can cut inside onto his strong foot to shoot.
- He was voted England's Men's Player of the Year in back-to-back seasons.
- After the Andorra game on his twentieth birthday, he gave his shirt to a young fan in the crowd.
- Arsenal's youth academy, Hale End, has produced several first-team players, but Bukayo is one of its biggest success stories.
- He's known for always smiling, even during tough training sessions.
