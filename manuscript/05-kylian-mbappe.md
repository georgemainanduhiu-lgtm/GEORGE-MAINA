# Chapter 5: Kylian Mbappé, The Kid with Posters on His Wall

> **FACT FILE**
>
> **Full name:** Kylian Mbappé Lottin
>
> **Born:** December 20, 1998, in Paris, France
>
> **Position:** Forward
>
> **Famous for:** Winning the World Cup at nineteen, scoring a hat-trick in a World Cup final, and becoming the all-time top scorer in World Cup history
>
> **Superpower:** Lightning speed, and the courage to bounce back from public heartbreak

## A Bedroom Full of Heroes

If you had walked into young Kylian Mbappé's bedroom in Bondy, a town just outside Paris, you would have seen posters. Lots of posters. They covered the walls from the floor almost to the ceiling.

And most of them showed the same player: **Cristiano Ronaldo.**

Kylian didn't just admire Ronaldo. He studied him. He watched how Ronaldo ran, how he shot, how he celebrated. He dreamed that one day he would play for Real Madrid, just like his hero.

Lots of kids have posters of soccer stars on their walls. But Kylian had something else that helped him chase that dream: a family that lived and breathed sports.

## A Sporty Family in Bondy

Kylian's dad, Wilfried, came from Cameroon and was a soccer coach at the local club, AS Bondy. His mom, Fayza, whose family has roots in Algeria, was a professional handball player. Kylian's older brother, Jirès, who grew up in the family, played professional soccer too. And his younger brother, Ethan, would one day also become a pro.

Bondy is a *banlieue*, a suburb of Paris, with tall apartment buildings and lots of concrete. Many families there don't have much money. But there are soccer fields everywhere, and kids play on them for hours. Kylian spent so much time at AS Bondy that it was almost his second home.

His coaches there said they had never seen anyone like him. He was faster than everyone. He could dribble with either foot. And he had a confidence that was rare for a little boy. When he was very young, he would tell people, calmly and seriously, that he was going to be a professional player.

Some people smiled at that. Lots of kids say they're going to be pros. But Kylian meant it.

## Saying "No" to the Big Clubs

As Kylian got older, the biggest clubs in Europe started to notice him. He went to Clairefontaine, France's famous national soccer academy, where many of the country's best young players train. He visited Chelsea in England when he was about eleven. When he was fourteen, he even got to visit Real Madrid, the club of his dreams, and meet its staff, including the French legend Zinedine Zidane.

You might think he would have jumped at the chance to join Real Madrid right then. But Kylian and his family made a careful decision. They wanted a club where he would get a real chance to play and grow, not just sit on the bench. So he signed for **AS Monaco**, a smaller club in the south of France known for giving young players a chance.

It was a smart choice. At Monaco, in December 2015, he made his first-team debut at sixteen years old, breaking a record held by the French legend Thierry Henry as the youngest player ever to play for the club. A few months later, he became Monaco's youngest-ever goal scorer, breaking another of Henry's records.

In the 2016–17 season, Kylian was eighteen, and he lit up Europe. Monaco won the French league title, beating the giant club Paris Saint-Germain, and reached the semifinals of the Champions League. Suddenly, everyone knew his name.

That summer, Paris Saint-Germain signed him. The kid from Bondy was coming home to the capital.

## World Champion at Nineteen

The 2018 World Cup in Russia was Kylian's first. He was nineteen years old.

In the round of 16, France played Argentina, with Lionel Messi. Kylian ran so fast that defenders couldn't keep up. He won a penalty and scored two goals. France won 4–3.

Then, in the final against Croatia (with Luka Modrić, from Chapter 3!), Kylian scored a long-range shot to help France win 4–2. He became only the second teenager ever to score in a World Cup final. The first was the Brazilian legend Pelé, sixty years earlier.

**France were world champions**, and Kylian was named the best young player of the tournament.

But here's the part of the story many people don't know. Kylian decided to give the money he earned from playing in the World Cup to a charity that helps children who are sick or have disabilities play sports. He was nineteen, a world champion, and he was already thinking about other kids.

## The Penalty in Bucharest

Kylian's career was flying. Then, in 2021, came one of the hardest nights of his life.

At the European Championship, France played Switzerland in the round of 16 in Bucharest, Romania. France were the big favorites. But the game ended 3–3 after extra time, and it went to a penalty shootout.

Nine players scored. Then it was Kylian's turn. If he missed, France would be out.

He stepped up. He shot. And the Swiss goalkeeper, Yann Sommer, dived and saved it.

France were knocked out. Kylian stood frozen on the field while the Swiss players celebrated.

What came next was even worse. Online, some people sent him cruel and racist messages. Some said he was arrogant. Some said he'd let the whole country down. Kylian was hurt, not just by the miss, but by the hate. He felt like he wasn't being protected. He later admitted that for a while, he thought about stepping away from the French national team.

Imagine being twenty-two and having millions of people blame you for something. Would you want to go back and try again?

Kylian did.

## The Greatest Hat-Trick That Wasn't Enough

The 2022 World Cup final in Qatar, Argentina vs. France, is still talked about as maybe the most exciting World Cup final ever.

For eighty minutes, France played badly. Argentina were winning 2–0. Some French fans had stopped watching. Lionel Messi was about to lift the trophy.

Then Kylian took over.

In the 80th minute, he scored a penalty. 2–1. Just over a minute later, he volleyed in a brilliant second goal. **2–2!** In extra time, Messi scored again to make it 3–2. And then, in the 118th minute, France won another penalty. Kylian stepped up once more, the same player who had missed in Bucharest, and he scored. **3–3!**

He had scored a **hat-trick** in a World Cup final, something nobody had done since 1966.

In the shootout, Kylian scored his penalty again. But some of his teammates missed, and Argentina won. Kylian sat on the grass, completely heartbroken. The President of France came down onto the field to comfort him.

He had lost the final. But he had shown the whole world something important: the player who missed in 2021 was not afraid to step up again when it mattered most.

## The Masked Captain

By Euro 2024, Kylian was France's captain. In the very first game, against Austria, he smashed his face into a defender's shoulder and **broke his nose**. Blood poured down his face, and he had to leave the field.

Most players would have been out for weeks. Kylian put on a special protective mask and kept playing. It was hot and uncomfortable, and it made it harder to see the ball at his feet. But he led France all the way to the semifinals. There, France lost to Spain, partly thanks to a stunning goal by a sixteen-year-old named Lamine Yamal (you'll meet him in Chapter 12).

Another disappointment. Another reason to come back stronger.

## Living the Dream (the Hard Way)

In 2024, Kylian finally achieved his childhood dream: he signed for Real Madrid, the club of his hero Cristiano Ronaldo. The boy with the posters on his wall was now wearing the same famous white shirt.

But dreams don't always start the way you imagine. His first months at Real Madrid were rough. He struggled to fit in with his new teammates. He missed two important penalties in a row, one against Liverpool and one against Athletic Bilbao. Critics said he was a flop. Some said he would never be as good in Madrid as he had been in Paris.

Kylian didn't hide. He kept working. He kept taking chances. And by the end of that first season, he had scored more than forty goals, including 31 in La Liga, enough to win the European Golden Shoe as the top scorer in all of Europe's leagues.

## The World Cup's Greatest Scorer

At the 2026 World Cup, Kylian was France's captain. In the very first game, against Senegal, he scored twice. Those goals made him **France's all-time top scorer**, passing the record of 57 goals that had belonged to Olivier Giroud.

He kept scoring. France lost a semifinal to Spain, which hurt. But in the match for third place against England, Kylian scored two more goals. That gave him **10 goals** at the tournament, enough to win the Golden Boot as the World Cup's top scorer, just like in 2022.

And it did something else. Those goals took Kylian to **22 World Cup goals** in his career, more than anyone in history. He passed the old record of Germany's Miroslav Klose. He even passed Lionel Messi, who had broken Klose's record earlier that summer.

The kid from Bondy was now the greatest goal scorer the World Cup had ever seen.

## Teamwork Spotlight: The Striker Who Didn't Score

Here's a fun puzzle. At the 2018 World Cup, France's starting striker played in every game, and France won the whole tournament. How many goals do you think he scored?

The answer is **zero**.

His name was Olivier Giroud. He was big and strong, and his job was to battle with defenders, win headers, hold up the ball, and drag defenders out of position. That created space for faster players like Kylian Mbappé and Antoine Griezmann to run into and score. Giroud didn't get the headlines. But the French coaches and players knew how important he was.

Years later, Giroud became France's all-time top scorer with 57 goals. And when people asked him whether Kylian would one day break his record, Giroud was generous. He said, of course Kylian would pass him, and that he hoped Kylian would reach a hundred goals for France.

At the 2026 World Cup, Kylian did pass him. It's a lovely example of what a good teammate looks like: someone who does the hard work nobody notices, and who's happy to see the next player go even further.

## Never-Give-Up Lesson

**A miss doesn't define you. What you do next does.**

Kylian Mbappé missed a penalty that knocked his country out of a major tournament, and he was attacked for it. He could have hidden. Instead, he kept stepping up to the penalty spot, including in a World Cup final. Courage isn't never failing. Courage is trying again after you fail, with the whole world watching.

## Fun Facts

- When Kylian scores, he often crosses his arms and tucks his hands under his armpits. It's a celebration his little brother Ethan came up with while playing a video game.
- Kylian has won the Golden Boot at two World Cups in a row: 2022 (8 goals) and 2026 (10 goals).
- He is one of the fastest players ever measured in a match. His top sprints have been timed at around 36 kilometers per hour (more than 22 miles per hour).
- Pelé and Kylian are the only two teenagers to have scored in a World Cup final.
- He can speak French, English, and Spanish.
