# Chapter 12: Lamine Yamal, The Baby in the Bathtub

> **FACT FILE**
>
> **Full name:** Lamine Yamal Nasraoui Ebana
>
> **Born:** July 13, 2007, in Esplugues de Llobregat, near Barcelona, Spain
>
> **Position:** Right winger
>
> **Famous for:** Winning the European Championship at sixteen, the World Cup at nineteen, and becoming one of the best players in the world while still a teenager
>
> **Superpower:** Playing with fearless joy, and never forgetting where he came from

## A Photo That Became Famous

In 2007, a photographer in Barcelona was taking pictures for a charity calendar. The idea was to show famous FC Barcelona players with ordinary local families.

For one of the photos, a young Barcelona player was paired with a local family who had a new baby. The player was twenty years old, shy, and still near the start of his career. His job was simple: help give the baby a bath while the camera clicked.

The player's name was **Lionel Messi**.

And the baby, just a few months old, splashing in a little plastic tub, was named **Lamine Yamal**.

Nobody in that room had any idea what they were part of. The young player would go on to become one of the greatest soccer players of all time. And the baby? The baby would grow up to become a world champion too, and in the 2026 World Cup final, he would play *against* the man who once gave him a bath.

You can't make this stuff up!

## 304

Lamine's father, Mounir, is from Morocco. His mother, Sheila, is from Equatorial Guinea, a small country in Central Africa. Lamine was born near Barcelona, and he spent much of his childhood in a neighborhood called **Rocafonda**, in the coastal town of Mataró.

Rocafonda is a working-class neighborhood. Many of the families there came from other countries, and most don't have a lot of money. But there were soccer courts and fenced-in concrete pitches, and that's where young Lamine spent his time. He played against older kids, bigger kids, and kids who didn't like losing to a little boy with fast feet.

Lamine never forgot Rocafonda. Today, when he scores a goal, he often makes a special celebration with his fingers: **3-0-4**. Those are the last three numbers of the postal code for his neighborhood, 08304. It's his way of telling the world: *This is where I come from. I'm proud of it.*

## Joining La Masia

When Lamine was about seven years old, scouts from FC Barcelona saw him play. They invited him to join the club's famous youth academy, **La Masia**.

Remember La Masia from Chapter 1? It's the same academy where Lionel Messi learned to play after moving from Argentina. It's produced so many great players that people sometimes call it a soccer factory. But only a small number of La Masia kids ever make it to Barcelona's first team.

Lamine spent years working his way up through the youth teams. Coaches loved how he could dribble past defenders with his left foot, almost always cutting inside from the right wing. They also noticed something else: he didn't seem to feel pressure. When the game was tight, he wanted the ball even more.

He also had to keep up with school. Lamine was a teenager, after all, and he still had classes, homework, and exams, just like you.

## Fifteen Years Old in La Liga

On April 29, 2023, Barcelona were playing Real Betis in La Liga. Late in the game, the coach looked down the bench and called a name: Lamine Yamal.

Lamine was **fifteen years and 290 days old**. When he jogged onto the field, he became the youngest player ever to play for Barcelona in La Liga.

The next season, the records kept tumbling. He became the youngest goal scorer in La Liga history. He made his debut for Spain's national team at sixteen and scored, becoming the youngest player ever to play for Spain and the youngest ever to score for them.

People everywhere started asking: how good could this kid become?

## Homework at the Euros

In the summer of 2024, Spain went to the European Championship in Germany. Lamine was sixteen, the youngest player in the whole tournament.

And while most of his teammates spent their free time relaxing, Lamine had something else to do: **homework**. He was in the middle of his school year, and he brought his schoolwork with him to the tournament so he wouldn't fall behind.

On the field, he was sensational. In the semifinal against France, Spain were losing 1–0. Lamine got the ball outside the penalty area, took a touch, and curled an incredible left-footed shot off the post and into the net. It was a goal that seemed impossible for someone so young.

At sixteen years and 362 days old, he became the youngest goal scorer in the history of the European Championship. Spain won 2–1.

In the final against England, played the day after his seventeenth birthday, Lamine helped set up Spain's first goal. Spain won 2–1 and became champions of Europe. Lamine was named the best young player of the tournament.

## Heartbreak Is Part of the Game

You might think that someone this talented never faces disappointment. But even Lamine has had to learn how to lose.

In the spring of 2025, Barcelona played Inter Milan in the semifinal of the Champions League. It was an incredible two-game battle. Lamine scored a wonderful goal in the first game, which ended 3–3. In the second game, Barcelona were so close to the final. But Inter scored a late equalizer and then won in extra time. Barcelona were knocked out, 7–6 over the two games.

A few weeks later, Spain lost the final of the Nations League to Portugal on penalties. Portugal's captain, Cristiano Ronaldo (Chapter 2!), scored the equalizer in that game.

Then, in September 2025, Lamine was nominated for the Ballon d'Or. He came second, behind Ousmane Dembélé (Chapter 6). Being voted the second-best player in the world at eighteen is amazing, but for someone who loves to win, it still stung.

And in the autumn of 2025, he got a painful groin injury that kept him out of games and made it hard for him to play like himself. Some critics started saying he was getting too much attention too soon.

Lamine took it all in. He listened to his coaches, worked on his recovery, and stayed focused on the field. He had something many people didn't see: a quiet, steady belief that the hard moments were part of the journey.

## The Number 10

In the summer of 2025, Barcelona gave Lamine a shirt with enormous meaning: the **number 10**.

Who wore Barcelona's number 10 for so many years? Lionel Messi.

The baby in the bathtub was now wearing the shirt of the man who had bathed him. And in the 2025–26 season, Lamine helped Barcelona win La Liga again, and he was voted the league's Player of the Season.

## Champion of the World

Then came the summer of 2026 and the World Cup in North America.

Lamine was still recovering from a muscle problem as the tournament began, but he was ready to play. He scored once and created chance after chance for his teammates. In the round of 16, Spain beat Cristiano Ronaldo's Portugal 1–0 with a goal in stoppage time.

In the semifinal, one day after his nineteenth birthday, Lamine faced Kylian Mbappé's France. Early in the game, he raced after a bouncing ball in the penalty area and won a penalty. Spain scored it and went on to win 2–0.

And then, on July 19, 2026, at MetLife Stadium in New Jersey, Spain played Argentina in the World Cup final.

Across the field stood Lionel Messi, now thirty-nine years old, playing in his sixth World Cup.

It was a tense, tough battle. After ninety minutes, the score was still 0–0. Lamine dribbled and probed and tried everything, but Argentina's goalkeeper, Emiliano Martínez, was making save after save. The game went to extra time.

In the 106th minute, Spain's Nico Williams, Lamine's best friend on the team, sent the ball across to Ferran Torres, who scored.

**Spain 1, Argentina 0.**

When the final whistle blew, Spain were **champions of the world** for the second time in their history. Lamine Yamal, the kid from Rocafonda, was a world champion at nineteen.

Somewhere, there's a photo of a twenty-year-old Messi holding a baby in a bathtub. Nineteen years later, the two of them had met in the biggest game in soccer. It was the kind of story that makes you believe in magic.

## The Story Isn't Finished

Lamine Yamal is still a teenager. Many people believe he will win the Ballon d'Or one day soon, maybe even by the time you read this book. But whatever trophies he wins, he still makes the 304 sign when he scores. He still talks about his mom and his neighborhood. And he still plays with the same joy he had on the concrete courts of Rocafonda.

His story is just getting started. And so is yours.

## Teamwork Spotlight: The Team Behind the Trophy

Lamine is a superstar. But look at who won the individual awards at the 2026 World Cup, and you'll see that Spain won as a **team**.

The Golden Ball, for the best player of the whole tournament, went to Spain's calm, clever midfielder **Rodri**. The award for the best young player went to **Pau Cubarsí**, a nineteen-year-old defender who, just like Lamine, came up through Barcelona's La Masia academy. He played every minute of every Spanish game. The Golden Glove, for the best goalkeeper, went to Spain's **Unai Simón**. And the winning goal in the final came from Ferran Torres, set up by Nico Williams.

Nico deserves a special mention. He and Lamine are best friends. They play on opposite wings, Lamine on the right and Nico on the left, and they often celebrate goals together with a special handshake. Nico scored in the Euro 2024 final with a pass from Lamine. Two years later, Nico made the pass that won the World Cup.

No single player wins a World Cup. Lamine knows that the dribbles and the goals only matter because of the defenders who win the ball, the midfielders who pass it, and the friends who are always making the run beside him.

## Never-Give-Up Lesson

**Stay humble, stay hungry, and remember where you came from.**

Lamine Yamal became famous incredibly young. He could have let it go to his head. Instead, he did his homework at the Euros, learned from his losses and injuries, and kept representing his neighborhood every time he scored. Big dreams are great, but remembering who you are is what keeps you going when things get tough.

## Fun Facts

- His "304" celebration comes from the postal code of his neighborhood, Rocafonda, in Mataró.
- Lamine won the Kopa Trophy, the award for the best player in the world under twenty-one, in both 2024 and 2025.
- He was named the best young player of Euro 2024, when he was still sixteen.
- He wears Barcelona's number 10 shirt, the same number Lionel Messi made famous there.
