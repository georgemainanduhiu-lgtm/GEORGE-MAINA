# Chapter 7: Vinícius Júnior, Keep Dancing

> **FACT FILE**
>
> **Full name:** Vinícius José Paixão de Oliveira Júnior
>
> **Born:** July 12, 2000, in São Gonçalo, Brazil
>
> **Position:** Left winger / forward
>
> **Famous for:** Scoring in two Champions League finals, winning FIFA's The Best Men's Player award in 2024, and standing up bravely against racism
>
> **Superpower:** Joy that nobody can take away from him

## The Boy Who Played with a Smile

Across the bay from the famous city of Rio de Janeiro in Brazil, there's a big, busy city called São Gonçalo. It's not the Rio you see on postcards, with beaches and mountains. Many families there work very hard just to get by. Vinícius Júnior, known to everyone as "Vini," grew up in one of those families.

Like millions of Brazilian kids, Vini fell in love with soccer before he could even read. He played on the streets, on concrete courts, and on any patch of ground he could find. And from the very beginning, there was something special about the way he played: **he smiled.** He danced with the ball. He did tricks just for the fun of it. When he scored, he celebrated like it was the happiest moment in the world.

In Brazil, that kind of joyful soccer has a name: *futebol alegria*, which means "happy football." Vini was full of it.

## Futsal and a Big Move

When he was about six, Vini started playing **futsal** at a school connected to Flamengo, the most popular club in Brazil. Futsal is a fast, indoor kind of soccer played on a hard court with a smaller, heavier ball. Because the space is so tight, futsal players have to think fast and control the ball perfectly. Many of Brazil's greatest players, like Ronaldinho and Neymar, grew up playing futsal.

Vini was brilliant at it. Soon, Flamengo wanted him in their main youth academy. But their training ground was far from São Gonçalo, a long and tiring journey. So when Vini was around ten years old, his family made a big decision. He went to live with his uncle Ulisses, who moved closer to Flamengo's training center so that Vini could get there more easily.

His family gave up a lot so that he could chase his dream. Vini never forgot that.

## Sold at Sixteen

Vini rose quickly through Flamengo's youth teams. In 2017, he starred for Brazil's under-17 team at the South American Championship, finishing as the top scorer and the best player as Brazil won the tournament.

That same year, when he was only sixteen, the biggest club in the world made its move. Real Madrid agreed to pay 45 million euros to sign him. He was so young that he had to wait until he turned eighteen before he could move to Spain. While he waited, he made his debut for Flamengo's first team and played in front of huge, roaring crowds at the Maracanã stadium.

In the summer of 2018, Vini packed his bags and flew to Madrid. He was eighteen, he didn't speak Spanish, and he was about to join a team full of world champions.

## "He Can't Shoot"

At first, Vini played for Real Madrid's reserve team. When he got his chance with the first team, fans loved his speed and his dribbling. He could beat defenders in a flash.

But there was a problem. When he got near the goal, he often missed. Shots flew over the bar. Shots went wide. Easy chances went begging.

People started laughing at him. On social media and on TV, they made fun of his finishing. Some people said Real Madrid had wasted their money on a kid who couldn't shoot. Videos of his misses were shared all over the internet.

It got so bad that in one game in 2020, cameras caught one of his own teammates, the French striker Karim Benzema, telling another player at halftime not to pass the ball to Vini.

Imagine how that felt. Not just strangers doubting you, but a teammate.

Vini could have given up. He could have asked to go back to Brazil. He could have stopped trying the tricky things that made him special.

Instead, he went to work.

## Practice, Practice, Practice

Vini spent hours after training practicing his shooting. He worked with coaches who studied videos of his chances and helped him learn when to shoot, where to aim, and how to stay calm in front of goal. He worked on his strength so defenders couldn't push him off the ball.

And something else happened, too. Instead of letting the Benzema moment become a war, the two players learned to understand each other. Over the next seasons, they became one of the best attacking partnerships in the world. Benzema set up Vini, and Vini set up Benzema. The striker who once said "don't pass to him" ended up passing to him again and again.

In the 2021–22 season, with the Italian coach Carlo Ancelotti guiding him, Vini exploded. He scored twenty-two goals in all competitions. The kid who "couldn't shoot" was suddenly one of the deadliest attackers in Europe.

## The Goal in Paris

On May 28, 2022, Real Madrid played Liverpool in the Champions League final in Paris.

Just before the hour mark, Real Madrid's Fede Valverde raced down the right side and fired the ball across the goal. And there, arriving at the far post, was Vini. He slid in and poked the ball into the net.

**Real Madrid 1, Liverpool 0.** That's how the game ended.

The boy whose finishing everyone used to laugh at had scored the winning goal in a Champions League final, at just twenty-one years old.

Two years later, in June 2024, he did it again, scoring in the Champions League final at London's Wembley Stadium as Real Madrid beat Borussia Dortmund 2–0.

## Standing Up When It Matters

Now we come to the bravest part of Vini's story.

Over several seasons in Spain, Vini was a target of **racism**. Racism is when people are treated badly, insulted, or hated because of the color of their skin or where they come from. At some stadiums, some fans shouted racist insults at Vini. Some made monkey noises. It happened again and again.

For a long time, many people in soccer didn't do enough to stop it.

Vini decided he wouldn't stay quiet. In May 2023, during a game in Valencia, some fans shouted racist abuse at him. Vini stopped. He walked toward the referee and pointed out exactly who was doing it. The game was stopped for several minutes.

After that game, Vini spoke out loudly and clearly. He said racism was a problem that needed to be fixed, and he wasn't going to pretend it wasn't happening.

Some people criticized him for speaking up. They said he should just "shut up and play." Some even said he was causing the problem by dancing when he scored.

But Vini kept speaking, and lots of people started listening. Players, clubs, and politicians around the world supported him. In Spain, people who had shouted racist abuse at him were taken to court, and in 2024, some of them were found guilty and punished. It was the first time that had ever happened in Spanish soccer. In Brazil, a law to fight racism at sports events was even nicknamed after him.

Vini had used his voice to make soccer better, not just for himself, but for every player who might face the same thing in the future.

## Never Stop Dancing

Through all of this, critics kept telling Vini to stop dancing when he scored. They said it was disrespectful. They said it made other teams angry.

Vini kept dancing.

His dancing isn't about showing off. It's about who he is: a happy kid from São Gonçalo who loves to play. When people tried to take that joy away from him, he held onto it even tighter. Teammates started dancing with him. Fans started a campaign telling him to keep dancing.

In 2024, Vini was voted FIFA's The Best Men's Player, the best player in the world for that year. He finished second in the Ballon d'Or voting.

He also started a charity in Brazil, the Instituto Vini Jr., which helps kids in public schools learn and succeed, using soccer to make learning fun. He wants children who grew up like he did to have every chance to chase their own dreams, in soccer or anything else.

## Ups and Downs

Vini's story is still being written, and it still has hard moments. In the 2025–26 season, Real Madrid didn't win a major trophy. At the 2026 World Cup, Brazil were knocked out in the round of 16 by Norway, thanks to two goals from a certain Norwegian striker (you'll meet Erling Haaland in Chapter 8).

But Vini kept going. He scored more than twenty goals that season, and in August 2026, he signed a new contract to stay at Real Madrid until 2032.

Wherever he goes next, one thing is certain: he'll keep dancing.

## Teamwork Spotlight: Standing Together

When Vini faced racist abuse in Valencia in 2023, he was not alone on the field.

His coach at the time, Carlo Ancelotti, spoke out strongly afterward. He said the problem wasn't Vini, it was the people who insulted him, and that soccer in Spain had a problem it needed to fix. Many of Vini's Real Madrid teammates posted messages supporting him. Players from other clubs, including some of Real Madrid's biggest rivals, did the same. Famous players from Brazil and around the world shared his story.

Vini also has a special friend in his teammate Rodrygo. Both grew up in Brazil, both came through Brazilian clubs as teenagers, and both moved to Real Madrid young. They understand each other's journey better than anyone. When one of them scores, you'll often see the other one dancing right beside him.

Standing up against unfairness is hard. It's much easier when other people stand next to you. That's why teammates, friends, and even strangers can make such a big difference: when you stand with someone who is being treated badly, you show them, and everyone watching, that they are not alone.

## Never-Give-Up Lesson

**Stand up for what's right, and don't let anyone steal your joy.**

When people laughed at Vini's shooting, he practiced until he was one of the best finishers in the world. When people insulted him because of his skin color, he stood up and spoke out, even when it was scary and some people told him to be quiet. And through it all, he kept the joy that made him love soccer in the first place.

If you see someone being treated unfairly, you can be brave like Vini: speak up, tell a trusted adult, and stand with them.

## Fun Facts

- Vini wears the number 7 shirt for Real Madrid, the same number Cristiano Ronaldo wore there.
- He won the Champions League with Real Madrid in 2022 and 2024, scoring in both finals.
- His charity, Instituto Vini Jr., created a learning app for schoolchildren in Brazil.
- He is a big fan of music and dancing, and many of his goal celebrations come from Brazilian dance styles.
- His coach at Real Madrid, Carlo Ancelotti, later became the head coach of Brazil's national team.
