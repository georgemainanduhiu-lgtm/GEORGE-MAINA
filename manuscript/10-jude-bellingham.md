# Chapter 10: Jude Bellingham, It's Never Too Late

> **FACT FILE**
>
> **Full name:** Jude Victor William Bellingham
>
> **Born:** June 29, 2003, in Stourbridge, England
>
> **Position:** Midfielder
>
> **Famous for:** Becoming a star at Birmingham City, Borussia Dortmund, and Real Madrid before age twenty-one, winning the Champions League, and scoring incredible last-minute goals for England
>
> **Superpower:** Believing the game is never over until the final whistle

## A Dad Who Scored 700 Goals

Jude Bellingham's dad, Mark, was a police officer. But on weekends, he was something else too: a goal-scoring machine.

Mark Bellingham played for small, local teams in what's called **non-league** soccer, the levels below the professional leagues. He never became famous. But over his long career, he scored more than **700 goals**. That's a lot of goals, even at that level!

So it's no surprise that Jude grew up with a ball at his feet. He and his younger brother, Jobe, played in the garden, in the park, and anywhere else they could find space. Their dad taught them to love the game, and their mum, Denise, drove them to practices and matches, week after week, year after year.

Jude was born in Stourbridge, a town in the West Midlands of England. When he was about seven, he joined the youth academy of the nearest big club, **Birmingham City**.

## Playing Up

At Birmingham, the coaches noticed something right away. Jude was too good for kids his own age.

So they did something that academies sometimes do with special talents: they made him **"play up."** That means playing with older kids, sometimes two or three years older.

Playing up isn't easy. The older boys were bigger, stronger, and faster. They didn't always like having a little kid on their team. Jude got knocked over. He got outrun. He had to learn to think faster, pass sooner, and be braver in tackles.

The coaches also had Jude play in lots of different positions: defense, midfield, attack. They wanted him to understand the whole game, not just one part of it.

It was hard. But it was exactly what made Jude special. By always being the youngest player on the field, he learned to handle pressure, to stay calm, and to compete with people who were bigger and stronger than him.

Years later, people would say that Jude Bellingham played like someone much older than his age. Now you know why!

## The Youngest Ever

On August 6, 2019, Jude made his debut for Birmingham City's first team. He was **sixteen years and thirty-eight days old**, which made him the youngest player ever to play for the club, breaking a record that had stood for decades.

A few weeks later, he scored his first goal, becoming the club's youngest-ever goal scorer, too.

He played forty-four games for Birmingham that season. He was so good that clubs all over Europe wanted to sign him. In the summer of 2020, at seventeen, he moved to Germany to play for Borussia Dortmund.

When he left, Birmingham City did something very unusual. They **retired his shirt number**, 22, so that no other player could wear it. Clubs usually only do that for legends who have played for many years. Some people thought it was silly to do that for a seventeen-year-old. Jude was going to prove that Birmingham had been right.

## Growing Up in Germany

Moving to another country at seventeen is a huge step. Jude didn't speak German. He didn't know anyone. But he didn't go alone: his mum, Denise, moved to Germany with him to help him settle in.

Jude worked hard to learn the language and to understand his new team. In his very first competitive game for Dortmund, he scored. Soon, he was a starter. By nineteen, he was sometimes wearing the captain's armband, an amazing honor for someone so young.

He also became the youngest player ever to play for England at a European Championship, coming on in the first game of Euro 2020 when he was still seventeen.

At the 2022 World Cup in Qatar, Jude, still only nineteen, scored England's first goal of the whole tournament, a powerful header against Iran. England reached the quarterfinals before losing a close game to France. Jude was learning that even when you play well, you don't always win. All you can do is give everything, and come back stronger.

## The Day the Title Slipped Away

In the 2022–23 season, Dortmund were fighting to win the German league, the Bundesliga, for the first time in more than ten years. Jude was their best player. He was so good that he was voted the Player of the Season.

On the last day of the season, Dortmund only needed to win their final game at home to be champions.

But Jude was injured and couldn't play. He had to watch from the stands. Dortmund drew 2–2. On the very same afternoon, their rivals, Bayern Munich, scored a late goal to win their game, and Bayern took the title instead.

Jude and his teammates were devastated. They had been so close, and it slipped away in the final minutes.

It was a painful lesson about how much the last few minutes of a season, or a game, can matter. Jude would remember it.

## Hey Jude!

In the summer of 2023, Real Madrid, the most successful club in Champions League history, signed Jude for more than 100 million euros. He was nineteen.

He was given the number 5 shirt, which had once been worn by the French legend Zinedine Zidane. And what did Jude do? He started scoring in almost every game.

He scored in his first four league games. In his first match against Barcelona, Real Madrid's biggest rivals, he scored twice, including the winning goal in stoppage time. In the return league match against Barcelona, a few months later, he scored *another* stoppage-time winner.

Real Madrid fans started singing "Hey Jude," the famous song by the Beatles, whenever he scored. At the end of that first season, Real Madrid won La Liga and the **Champions League**. The final was played at Wembley Stadium, in London, so Jude got to win Europe's biggest trophy in his home country.

## Playing Through the Pain

What most fans didn't know was that for much of that time, Jude was hurting.

In November 2023, he dislocated his shoulder in a game, meaning the bone popped out of its socket. It is very painful. Usually, a player might have an operation and miss months of games. But Jude didn't want to miss games for his team, so he kept playing, wearing a special brace to hold his shoulder in place. His shoulder kept slipping out again and again. He played like that for well over a year.

Finally, in the summer of 2025, he had surgery to fix it properly. Then he went through months of hard, boring recovery work before he could play again.

## The Bicycle Kick

Now let's go back to the summer of 2024, and one of the most famous goals in England's history.

It was the round of 16 at the European Championship in Germany. England were playing Slovakia, and England were losing 1–0. The clock ticked past 90 minutes. England were about to be knocked out. Some English fans had started to leave the stadium.

In the fifth minute of stoppage time, England took a long throw-in. The ball was flicked on into the penalty area, and it floated in the air near Jude, with his back to the goal.

Most players would have tried to control it. Jude did something else. He leaped into the air, threw his legs up over his head, and hit a **bicycle kick**.

The ball flew into the net.

**1–1!**

It was one of the latest goals England had ever scored to save themselves at a major tournament. England went on to win the game in extra time and reached the final, where they lost to Spain.

Remember the day the Bundesliga title slipped away in the final minutes? Jude had learned that the last minutes can break your heart. Now he knew they could also be where miracles happen, if you never stop believing.

## Again, When It Mattered Most

At the 2026 World Cup, England faced Erling Haaland's Norway (from Chapter 8) in the quarterfinal. Norway took the lead. England were struggling.

Just before halftime, in stoppage time, Jude fired in a left-footed shot to tie the game. Then, in extra time, he scored *again* to win it 2–1. England were in the semifinal!

England lost that semifinal to Argentina in the final minutes, which was a tough way to go out. But they bounced back to beat France in the third-place game, finishing third: England's best World Cup since 1966.

## Teamwork Spotlight: The Bellingham Team

Jude's first team wasn't Birmingham City. It was his family.

His dad, Mark, taught him the game and showed him what it means to love scoring goals. His mum, Denise, drove him to training and matches for years, and then moved to a whole new country with him when he was seventeen so he wouldn't be alone. His little brother, Jobe, was his first opponent in the back garden, and he grew up to be a professional player, too.

On the field, Jude has had great teammates at every stop. At Dortmund, he played alongside Erling Haaland (Chapter 8), and the two became good friends. At Real Madrid, he joined Vinícius Júnior (Chapter 7) and, later, Kylian Mbappé (Chapter 5). For England, he plays with Bukayo Saka (Chapter 9) and the team's captain and record goal scorer, Harry Kane, who scored the winning goal in extra time after Jude's famous bicycle kick.

Jude is known as a leader who talks a lot on the field, encouraging teammates, pointing out where they should run, and demanding that everyone give their best. Being a good teammate doesn't mean being quiet. Sometimes it means being the loudest voice of belief in the room.

## Never-Give-Up Lesson

**It's never over until the final whistle.**

Jude Bellingham learned early, by playing against older kids, that hard challenges make you stronger. He learned at Dortmund that a game, or a season, can be lost in the last few minutes. And then he made a habit of turning those last few minutes into his own moments of magic. When everyone else thinks it's over, the never-give-up player keeps going.

## Fun Facts

- When Jude scores, he often celebrates by standing tall with his arms stretched out wide.
- His younger brother, Jobe, is also a professional soccer player and joined Borussia Dortmund, Jude's old club, in 2025.
- Birmingham City retired his number 22 shirt when he was just seventeen.
- He won the Golden Boy award in 2023 as the best young player in Europe.
- He was La Liga's Player of the Season in his very first year in Spain.
