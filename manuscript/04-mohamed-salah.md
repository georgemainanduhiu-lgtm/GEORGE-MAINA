# Chapter 4: Mohamed Salah, The Long Bus Ride to Glory

> **FACT FILE**
>
> **Full name:** Mohamed Salah Hamed Mahrous Ghaly
>
> **Born:** June 15, 1992, in Nagrig, Egypt
>
> **Position:** Right winger / forward
>
> **Famous for:** Breaking scoring records at Liverpool, winning two Premier League titles and the Champions League, and becoming a hero to a whole nation
>
> **Superpower:** Patience, and the willingness to go the extra mile (literally!)

## A Village by the Nile

In the green farmland of the Nile Delta in northern Egypt, there's a small village called Nagrig. Farmers grow crops there, and the air sometimes smells of jasmine flowers. The streets are narrow and dusty. And in those streets, a skinny boy named Mohamed, called "Mo" for short, played soccer from morning until night.

Mo's heroes were the great players he watched on TV: Zinedine Zidane, Francesco Totti, and the Brazilian striker Ronaldo. He would race through the village with the ball at his feet, pretending to be them, imagining crowds roaring his name.

But there was a problem. Nagrig was a tiny village. There were no professional soccer academies nearby. If Mo wanted to become a real player, he would have to go somewhere much bigger.

## Five Buses, Every Day

When Mo was about fourteen, he joined the youth team of a professional club called Al Mokawloon, which is based in Egypt's enormous capital city, Cairo.

There was just one problem: Cairo was far away from Nagrig. Really far.

To get to practice, Mo had to leave school early. Then he took a bus. Then he changed to another bus. Then another. Depending on the day, it could take as many as five different buses to get there. The journey took around four hours, sometimes more. Then he would train for two hours. Then he would ride all those buses back home again, arriving late at night, exhausted.

The next morning, he'd get up and do it all again. Five days a week. For years.

Think about that the next time you don't feel like walking to practice! Mo spent eight or nine hours a day traveling, all for a chance to play. He was tired. Sometimes he was hungry. He often had to do his homework on the bus. But he never stopped going.

## The Boy Who Cried After Winning

Here's a surprising fact: when Mo first joined Al Mokawloon's youth team, he didn't play as a forward. The coaches put him at **left back**, which is a defender's position.

One day, his team won a game 4–0. Everyone was celebrating. But Mo was sitting by himself, crying.

His coach was confused. "We won!" he said. "Why are you crying?"

Mo was upset because he'd had chances to score and missed them. He didn't just want to win. He wanted to score goals. The coach realized that a defender who cries because he didn't score probably isn't really a defender. So he moved Mo forward, where he belonged.

It was one of the best decisions in soccer history.

## A Sad Day, and an Unexpected Chance

By the time he was seventeen, Mo was playing for Al Mokawloon's first team in the Egyptian Premier League. He was fast, tricky, and he loved to cut in from the right side and shoot with his left foot.

Then, in February 2012, something terrible happened in Egyptian soccer. At a stadium in the city of Port Said, a riot broke out after a match, and more than seventy fans lost their lives. The whole country was in mourning. The Egyptian league was stopped, and it didn't start again that season.

For young players like Mo, it was a scary time. With no games to play, how would anyone ever see how good he was?

To help keep the young players fit, Egypt's under-23 team arranged a friendly match in Switzerland against a club called FC Basel. Mo came on at halftime, and he was amazing. He found the net and ran Basel's defenders ragged.

Basel's coaches couldn't believe what they'd seen. They invited him to train with them, and soon after, they signed him.

Out of a terrible time came an unexpected opportunity. Mo was going to Europe.

## Cold, Lonely, and Far from Home

Switzerland was very different from Nagrig. It was cold, sometimes snowy. People spoke German and French, and Mo didn't speak either language. He didn't speak much English yet, either. The food was different. He didn't know anybody.

He felt lonely, and it was hard to talk to his teammates. But Mo kept a positive attitude. He studied languages. He listened carefully to his coaches. And on the field, he let his speed and skill do the talking. He helped Basel win the Swiss league twice. He even scored against the big English club Chelsea, more than once.

Chelsea were so impressed that, in January 2014, they bought him.

## The Bench at Chelsea

Moving to Chelsea, one of the richest clubs in the Premier League, should have been a dream come true. Instead, it was one of the hardest times of Mo's career.

He hardly got to play. The manager preferred other players. Week after week, Mo sat on the bench or didn't make the team at all. Some people decided he wasn't good enough for the Premier League. They called him a "flop."

Mo could have given up on his dream of shining in England. Instead, he went to Italy, first on loan to Fiorentina and then to AS Roma. And in Italy, he exploded. He scored goal after goal and became one of the most exciting players in the country.

He was learning something important: sometimes you have to take a step to the side before you can take a giant step forward.

## The Egyptian King

In 2017, Liverpool signed Mo Salah. Some fans weren't sure. "Isn't he the guy who didn't make it at Chelsea?" they asked.

Mo answered in the best possible way. In his first season at Liverpool, he scored **32 goals in the Premier League**. That was a new record for a 38-game Premier League season (until a certain Norwegian striker broke it, but you'll read about him in Chapter 8). Overall that season, he scored 44 goals. Liverpool fans started singing songs about him, calling him "the Egyptian King."

That same year, he did something that made him a hero to more than a hundred million Egyptians.

## The Penalty for a Nation

Egypt had not played in a World Cup since 1990, before Mo was even born. In October 2017, they played the Republic of the Congo in a qualifying match. If Egypt won, they would go to the 2018 World Cup.

The game was tied 1–1 in the final minutes. Egypt's fans were in agony. Then, deep in stoppage time, in the 95th minute, Egypt won a penalty.

Mo Salah picked up the ball. The whole country held its breath.

He scored.

All over Egypt, people poured into the streets, dancing, singing, and honking car horns until late into the night. After 28 years, Egypt were going back to the World Cup, thanks to the boy from Nagrig.

## Hurt in Kyiv, Healed in Madrid

In 2018, Liverpool reached the Champions League final against Real Madrid in Kyiv, Ukraine. It was the biggest game of Mo's life.

About half an hour in, he was pulled to the ground in a tackle with Real Madrid's captain, Sergio Ramos, and landed hard on his shoulder. He tried to keep going, but he couldn't. He walked off the field in tears. Liverpool lost the final 3–1.

The shoulder injury also made it harder for Mo at the World Cup that summer, and Egypt lost all three of their games.

Mo was heartbroken. But he didn't let it define him. One year later, in June 2019, Liverpool were back in the Champions League final, this time against Tottenham in Madrid. In the **second minute**, Liverpool got a penalty. Mo stepped up and blasted it into the net. Liverpool won 2–0 and became champions of Europe.

The next season, Mo helped Liverpool win the Premier League for the first time in thirty years. And in the 2024–25 season, he did it again, winning a second Premier League title while scoring 29 league goals and setting up 18 more. That season, he won the PFA Players' Player of the Year award for the third time, becoming the first person ever to win it three times.

## Never Forgetting Home

Mo Salah became one of the most famous people in the world. But he never forgot Nagrig.

He has given money to build and improve a school, a hospital unit, and other things his village needed, and he has helped many families there. The school he went to as a boy was even renamed in his honor.

He has also shown kids everywhere that you can be proud of where you come from, wherever you go.

## A New Beginning

In the summer of 2026, Mo led Egypt at the World Cup in North America. Egypt made it out of the group stage. Then, in the round of 32, they beat Australia in a penalty shootout, Egypt's first-ever win in a World Cup knockout game!

In the next round, they faced Lionel Messi's Argentina. Amazingly, Egypt took a 2–0 lead! But Argentina came roaring back to win 3–2. Afterward, Mo told his teammates how proud he was of them. He said this wasn't the end for Egyptian soccer, but the beginning of something bigger.

That summer, after nine seasons, Mo also said goodbye to Liverpool, as one of the top three goal scorers in the club's long history. He left to start a new adventure, but the songs about the Egyptian King are still sung at Liverpool's stadium, Anfield.

Not bad for a boy who used to ride five buses to practice.

## Teamwork Spotlight: The Front Three

When Liverpool won the Champions League in 2019 and the Premier League in 2020, Mo Salah wasn't the only attacker making it happen.

Liverpool's manager, Jürgen Klopp, built his team around a famous front three: Mo Salah on the right, Sadio Mané on the left, and Roberto Firmino in the middle. Each of them was brilliant in a different way. Mo was the goal scorer. Mané was explosive and fast. Firmino was the clever one who dropped back, ran tirelessly, and set up his teammates. He often didn't care whether he scored, as long as the team did.

The three of them pressed the other team's defenders like a pack of wolves, chasing every ball. Behind them, the giant defender Virgil van Dijk kept things solid at the back.

Klopp also believed in Mo when some people still remembered his tough days at Chelsea. Klopp gave him confidence and freedom, and Mo paid him back with goal after goal.

Mo's records are amazing. But he always says the team comes first. Every goal he scores starts with someone winning the ball, someone making a pass, and someone making a run to pull a defender out of the way. Nobody scores alone.

## Never-Give-Up Lesson

**Be willing to go the extra mile. And when one door closes, find another.**

Mo traveled eight hours a day just to train. When he didn't get a chance at Chelsea, he went to Italy and found one. When he got hurt in the biggest final of his life, he came back a year later and won it. He kept moving forward, even when the path wasn't straight.

## Fun Facts

- Mo is left-footed, even though he usually plays on the right wing. That lets him cut inside and curl shots into the far corner.
- His daughter is named Makka, after the holy city of Mecca. She has sometimes appeared on the field with him after big wins.
- In 2019, he was named one of *TIME* magazine's 100 most influential people in the world.
- He has been named African Footballer of the Year more than once.
- He has won the Premier League Golden Boot, for scoring the most goals in a season, four times: in 2017–18, 2018–19, 2021–22, and 2024–25. (Twice he shared it with other players who scored just as many.)
