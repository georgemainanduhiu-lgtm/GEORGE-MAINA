# Chapter 1: Lionel Messi, The Boy Who Was Too Small

> **FACT FILE**
>
> **Full name:** Lionel Andrés Messi
>
> **Born:** June 24, 1987, in Rosario, Argentina
>
> **Position:** Forward / playmaker
>
> **Famous for:** Eight Ballon d'Or awards, winning the 2022 World Cup with Argentina, and dribbling past defenders like they're standing still
>
> **Superpower:** Never, ever letting go of a dream

## The Smallest Player on the Field

In a dusty neighborhood in Rosario, Argentina, a grandmother named Celia had a problem. Her grandson Lionel would not stop kicking a ball. He kicked it in the kitchen. He kicked it in the street. He kicked it against walls until the sun went down.

One day, Celia took him to watch his older brothers play at a local club called Grandoli. The team was one player short. The coach looked around and saw only one extra kid: a tiny boy, about four years old, who looked far too small to play.

Celia didn't hesitate. "Put him in," she said.

The coach wasn't sure. But Grandma Celia insisted. So the coach agreed. If the little boy cried or got scared, he'd take him right off.

The first time the ball came to Lionel, it bounced past him. He just watched it go. But the second time, something amazing happened. Lionel trapped the ball, put his head down, and ran. He dribbled past one kid. Then another. Then another. The players on the field were much bigger than him, but they couldn't get the ball away. It was as if the ball were glued to his left foot.

The coach never took him off.

Lionel's grandmother became his biggest fan. She took him to practice, cheered at every game, and told everyone that one day her "Leo" would be a star. Sadly, Grandma Celia died when Leo was ten years old. He was heartbroken. But he found a way to keep her close. Even today, when Messi scores a goal, he often points both fingers up to the sky. That's for her.

## A Machine Made of Kids

Leo's dad, Jorge, worked at a steel factory. His mom, Celia (named the same as his grandma), worked too. The family didn't have much money, but they had a lot of love, and a lot of soccer.

When Leo was six, he joined the youth teams of Newell's Old Boys, one of the most famous clubs in Rosario. His team was so good that people gave it a nickname: "The Machine of '87," because all the boys were born in 1987. They won game after game and hardly ever lost. Lionel, the smallest of them all, was the star.

He could do tricks for an hour without letting the ball touch the ground. At halftime of grown-up matches, he would go out on the field and juggle while the crowd clapped. Everyone in Rosario started to hear about the little magician.

But there was one problem that nobody could ignore.

Leo wasn't growing.

## The Night Injections

When Lionel was about ten, the other boys were getting taller every year. Leo stayed tiny. His parents took him to a doctor, who did tests and found the reason. Leo's body wasn't making enough of something called **growth hormone**. Growth hormone is a natural chemical that tells your bones and muscles to grow. Without it, Leo might stay very, very small for the rest of his life.

There was a treatment, but it wasn't easy. Every night, Leo would need a shot of medicine in his legs. Every single night, for years.

Can you imagine giving yourself a shot before bed, night after night? Leo learned to do it himself. He kept the medicine in the fridge and the needles in a little kit, and he never complained. He knew why he was doing it. He wanted to play soccer, and he wanted to be the best.

But the medicine was very expensive: around a thousand dollars every month. That was far more than the Messi family could afford. Jorge asked the clubs in Argentina for help. Some of them were interested in Leo, but no club would promise to pay for the treatment for as long as he needed it.

For a while, it seemed like Lionel's dream might end right there. Not because he wasn't good enough, but because of money.

Then a door opened, on the other side of the world.

## The Contract on a Napkin

Some people who knew about Leo's talent told FC Barcelona about him. Barcelona is one of the biggest clubs in the world, in Spain. In September 2000, thirteen-year-old Lionel and his father flew across the Atlantic Ocean for a trial.

When the coaches saw him play, their mouths dropped open. The tiny boy from Argentina was dancing around players who were bigger and older. One of the top people at the club, Carles Rexach, knew right away that Barcelona needed to sign him.

But other people at the club weren't sure. He was so small. He was so far from home. Paying for his medicine would cost money. Weeks went by and nobody decided anything. Leo's family started to worry that it would never happen.

Finally, in December 2000, Rexach was sitting in a restaurant with Leo's representatives. Everyone was tired of waiting. So Rexach grabbed the closest piece of paper he could find, which was a **paper napkin**, and wrote down a promise: Barcelona would sign Lionel Messi.

That napkin became one of the most famous pieces of paper in sports history. Barcelona agreed to pay for Leo's treatment. And in early 2001, the Messi family packed up their lives and moved to Spain.

## Lonely in a New Land

Moving to a new country sounds exciting, but for Leo it was very hard.

He was a shy boy, and he barely talked. In Barcelona, people spoke differently, ate different food, and did things in different ways. His mom and his brothers and sister missed Argentina so much that they decided to move back home. Leo stayed in Spain with his dad. Some nights he felt so homesick that he cried.

On the field, things weren't easy either. At first, paperwork problems between the clubs meant Leo could hardly play in official games. Then he broke his leg. For a boy who only wanted to play, it was miserable.

But Leo didn't quit. He kept taking his shots at night. He kept practicing. He made friends with other boys at the Barcelona academy, which is called **La Masia**. Two of them, Cesc Fàbregas and Gerard Piqué, became future world champions too. Together, people called them the "Baby Dream Team," because they won almost everything.

And slowly, very slowly, Lionel grew. The treatment worked. He never became a giant, but he grew to about 5 feet 7 inches (around 1.70 meters), which was plenty tall enough. And because he was still smaller than most players, he had something they didn't: a low center of gravity that let him twist and turn faster than anyone.

His "problem" had become his secret weapon.

## A Ride on Ronaldinho's Back

In October 2004, at just seventeen years old, Lionel Messi played his first official game for Barcelona's first team.

A few months later, on May 1, 2005, the ball came to him from the club's biggest superstar, a Brazilian genius named Ronaldinho. Messi chipped the ball gently over the goalkeeper and into the net. It was his first official goal for Barcelona.

Ronaldinho was so happy that he picked Leo up and carried him on his back like a big brother. It was as if he were saying to the world: *"Watch this kid. He's going to be the best."*

He was right.

Over the next sixteen years, Messi became the most important player in Barcelona history. He scored more goals for the club than anyone ever had, over 600 of them. He won La Liga ten times and the Champions League four times. He won the **Ballon d'Or**, the prize for the best player in the world, again and again, until he had won it **eight times**, more than anyone in history.

You might think that someone that successful never had to fight through hard times again.

You would be wrong.

## Losing, and Losing Again

Lionel Messi wanted one thing more than anything: to win a big trophy for his country, Argentina.

But it seemed like that dream was cursed.

His very first game for Argentina's senior team, in 2005, lasted about two minutes. He came on as a substitute, a defender grabbed his shirt, Leo tried to shake him off, and the referee sent him off with a red card. He cried in the locker room.

That was only the beginning.

- In 2007, Argentina reached the final of the **Copa América** (South America's biggest tournament) and lost.
- In 2014, Argentina reached the **World Cup** final in Brazil. The game went into extra time. Then Germany scored, and Argentina lost 1–0. Messi had to walk past the World Cup trophy, so close he could have touched it, without being allowed to lift it.
- In 2015, Argentina reached the Copa América final again. They lost on penalties.
- In 2016, Argentina reached the Copa América final *again*. And they lost on penalties *again*. This time, Messi missed his own penalty kick.

Four finals. Four heartbreaks. Some people in Argentina said cruel things. They said Messi only played well for Barcelona. They said he didn't care about his country.

After the 2016 final, Leo sat on the bench with his head in his hands. Then he said something shocking. He said that for him, the national team was over. He was quitting.

## "Don't Go, Leo"

Argentina went into shock. Fans who had criticized him realized how much they would miss him. Kids wrote him letters. People gathered in the streets with signs that said, *"No te vayas, Lio"*, which is Spanish for **"Don't go, Leo."** Even the president of Argentina called and asked him to stay.

Leo thought about it. He thought about how much the shirt meant to him. And just a few weeks later, he changed his mind. He came back.

Think about how brave that was. It would have been so much easier to stay away and protect himself from more pain. Instead, he chose to try again, knowing he might fail again.

For five more years, Argentina kept trying. Then, in July 2021, Argentina reached the Copa América final against their biggest rivals, Brazil, inside Brazil's most famous stadium, the Maracanã. Argentina won 1–0.

When the final whistle blew, Messi dropped to his knees and covered his face. His teammates ran to him. Not to celebrate with each other, but to lift *him* into the air, again and again. They knew how long he'd waited.

## The Greatest Final Ever

Then came the 2022 World Cup in Qatar. Messi was thirty-five. Everyone knew it might be his last real chance.

And in the very first game, Argentina lost to Saudi Arabia, one of the biggest shocks in World Cup history.

Messi could have panicked. Instead, he told the fans to trust the team. And Argentina didn't lose again. They beat Mexico, Poland, Australia, the Netherlands, and Croatia to reach the final against France.

The final, played on December 18, 2022, is often called the greatest World Cup final ever played. Argentina led 2–0. Then France's young superstar, Kylian Mbappé (you'll meet him later in this book!), scored two goals in less than two minutes. Messi scored again in extra time, 3–2! Then Mbappé scored again, 3–3! The game went to a penalty shootout.

Messi stepped up first for Argentina. He scored. His teammates scored. Argentina's goalkeeper, Emiliano Martínez, made a save. And then Gonzalo Montiel smashed in the winning penalty.

**Argentina were world champions.**

Messi had scored twice in the final and seven goals in the tournament, and he won the Golden Ball for best player. The little boy who once needed injections to grow was standing on top of the world, holding the trophy he'd walked past in 2014.

## Still Going

Messi didn't stop there. He won another Copa América with Argentina in 2024. He moved to the United States to play for Inter Miami, where he won the club's very first trophy and later led them to their first MLS Cup final.

And in the summer of 2026, at thirty-nine years old, he played in the World Cup *again*. It was his sixth World Cup, which made him one of the first two men ever to play in six (the other was Cristiano Ronaldo, who's in Chapter 2!). During that tournament, he played his 200th game for Argentina and broke the old record for the most World Cup goals ever. He led his team all the way to the final once more.

In that final, Argentina lost 1–0 in extra time to Spain, a team with a nineteen-year-old superstar named Lamine Yamal. And here's an amazing twist: when Lamine was a tiny baby, a twenty-year-old Lionel Messi held him in a bathtub for a charity photo shoot. (You'll find out the whole story in Chapter 12.)

Messi didn't win that final. But he was voted the second-best player of the whole tournament, at thirty-nine years old. More importantly, he showed the world one more time what he'd been showing it since he was four:

You keep playing. You keep trying. Win or lose, you never give up on your dream.

## Teamwork Spotlight: The Little Passers

People often talk about Messi as if he did everything alone. But Leo himself would be the first to tell you that he didn't.

At Barcelona, two midfielders who also grew up at La Masia, Xavi Hernández and Andrés Iniesta, became his perfect partners. They were small, too, and they loved to pass the ball: short, quick passes, again and again, until the other team got dizzy chasing it. Fans called the style *tiki-taka*. With Xavi and Iniesta feeding him the ball, Messi scored goal after goal. In 2009, Barcelona won **six trophies** in a single year, something no club had ever done before.

And Messi passed back, too. He has set up hundreds of goals for teammates during his career, and at the 2026 World Cup he became the player with the most assists in World Cup history.

When Argentina finally won the 2022 World Cup, his teammates said they were playing "for Leo." Ángel Di María scored in the final. Emiliano Martínez made a famous save in the last minute of extra time. Messi was the captain, but he needed every single one of them. The greatest players make the people around them better, and the people around them make the greatest players possible.

## Never-Give-Up Lesson

**Your "weakness" might become your superpower.**

Leo Messi was told he was too small. He had to take shots every night just to grow. But being small made him quick, balanced, and almost impossible to knock over. And losing four finals in a row didn't destroy him. It made his victories mean even more.

What do people say is your weakness? Maybe, with hard work, it can become your strength.

## Fun Facts

- Barcelona's famous "napkin contract" was signed on December 14, 2000.
- Messi is left-footed. Most of his greatest goals were scored with his left foot.
- When he was a kid, he was so quiet that his teammates sometimes forgot he was in the room, until he got the ball.
- He has won the Ballon d'Or eight times: in 2009, 2010, 2011, 2012, 2015, 2019, 2021, and 2023.
- At the 2026 World Cup, he also set a new record for the most assists in World Cup history.
