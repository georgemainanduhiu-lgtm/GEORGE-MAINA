# Chapter 2: Cristiano Ronaldo, The Island Boy Who Outworked Everyone

> **FACT FILE**
>
> **Full name:** Cristiano Ronaldo dos Santos Aveiro
>
> **Born:** February 5, 1985, in Funchal, on the island of Madeira, Portugal
>
> **Position:** Forward
>
> **Famous for:** Five Ballon d'Or awards, five Champions League titles, and scoring more goals for his country than any other man in history
>
> **Superpower:** A work ethic that never takes a day off

## The Boy Who Cried

Far out in the Atlantic Ocean, about 600 miles (nearly 1,000 kilometers) from the coast of Portugal, there's a green, mountainous island called Madeira. In its biggest town, Funchal, a boy named Cristiano grew up in a tiny house with his mom, his dad, his big brother, and his two big sisters.

The family didn't have much money. Cristiano's mom, Dolores, worked long hours as a cook and a cleaner. His dad, José Dinis, worked as a gardener for the town and helped out at a small local soccer club called Andorinha, where he looked after the team's kit. Cristiano was named after a famous American: his dad's favorite actor, Ronald Reagan, who later became President of the United States.

Cristiano loved soccer so much that he sometimes climbed out of his bedroom window to go and play, even when he was supposed to be doing homework. He played in the streets with a ball made of rolled-up socks when he didn't have a real one.

But here's something that might surprise you. The kids at Andorinha gave young Cristiano two nicknames. One was "Little Bee," because he was so quick and buzzed around the field. The other one was **"Cry Baby."**

Why? Because Cristiano hated to lose. If his team lost, he cried. If a teammate didn't pass him the ball, he cried. If he made a mistake, he cried. He cared so much that the feelings just came pouring out.

Some people might think that's a weakness. But that burning desire to win would one day turn Cristiano Ronaldo into one of the greatest players who ever lived.

## Alone in the Big City

When Cristiano was twelve, one of the biggest clubs in Portugal, Sporting Lisbon, offered him a place in their academy.

There was just one catch. Lisbon was on the mainland, far away across the ocean. Cristiano would have to leave his family and move there **alone**.

Imagine being twelve and saying goodbye to your mom at the airport, knowing you'd only see her a few times a year. Cristiano did it. And at first, it was awful.

He was so homesick that he called home crying. He missed his mom's cooking and his friends. In Lisbon, some kids laughed at him because of the way he talked. People from Madeira have a different accent, and the other boys made fun of it. Phone calls home were expensive, so he couldn't even talk to his family as often as he wanted.

Lots of kids would have packed their bags and gone home. Cristiano thought about it. But then he made a decision. If he was going to be away from his family, he would make it worth it. He would become the best.

He started sneaking out of bed at night to go to the gym. He tied weights around his ankles so that when he took them off, he would feel faster. He practiced dribbling with weights. He stayed late after training to practice free kicks, over and over, until the sun went down.

## A Problem with His Heart

When Cristiano was fifteen, something scary happened. During a medical test, doctors found that his heart was beating much too fast, even when he was resting. It was a condition called **tachycardia**.

For an athlete, a heart problem can mean the end of a career. Cristiano was terrified that his dream was over before it had really begun.

Doctors decided to fix the problem with an operation using a laser. Cristiano's mom was very worried. But the surgery worked. And just a few days later, Cristiano was back on the training field, running as hard as ever.

He had been given a second chance, and he wasn't going to waste it.

## The Game That Changed Everything

At seventeen, Cristiano made his debut for Sporting's first team. Just a week after his first league game, he scored two goals in a match, including an amazing solo run past several defenders.

Then, in August 2003, Sporting opened their brand-new stadium with a friendly match against the famous English club Manchester United. Eighteen-year-old Cristiano ran rings around United's defenders. He dribbled, he spun, he flicked the ball over their heads. The United players were so impressed that on the flight home, they kept telling their manager, Sir Alex Ferguson, that he *had* to sign this kid.

Ferguson agreed. A few days later, Cristiano Ronaldo was a Manchester United player. He was given the club's most famous shirt: **number 7**, which had once been worn by legends like George Best, Eric Cantona, and David Beckham.

That's a lot of pressure for an eighteen-year-old.

## Kicked, Booed, and Better

England's Premier League was rough. Defenders were big and strong, and they didn't like a skinny teenager doing fancy tricks around them. They kicked him. They knocked him down. Some fans and reporters said Cristiano was a show-off who did too many step-overs and fell down too easily.

In 2004, Portugal hosted the European Championship. Cristiano, now nineteen, helped his country reach the final. But Portugal lost 1–0 to Greece, at home, in front of their own fans. Cristiano walked off the field in tears.

The next year brought something even harder. In 2005, Cristiano's father died after a long illness. His dad had struggled with drinking too much alcohol, and it had damaged his health. Cristiano was crushed. He made a promise to himself: he would never drink alcohol. He has kept that promise.

Then, at the 2006 World Cup, Portugal played England, and Cristiano was involved in a moment that made many English fans angry with him. When he came back to Manchester United, he got booed at stadiums all over England.

Cristiano could have run away from England. Instead, he answered with his feet.

He went to the gym more. He ate healthier. He listened to his coaches and learned to pass at the right moment, not just dribble. He turned himself from a skinny winger into a powerful goal machine. In the 2007–08 season, he scored 42 goals.

In May 2008, Manchester United played Chelsea in the Champions League final in Moscow. The game went to a penalty shootout, and Cristiano, the team's superstar, **missed his kick**. For a few awful minutes, he thought he had cost his team the trophy. But his teammates had his back. Chelsea missed too, and United's goalkeeper Edwin van der Sar made the final save.

Cristiano fell face-down on the wet grass and cried. This time they were happy tears. Later that year, he won his first Ballon d'Or as the best player in the world.

Teamwork had saved him. He never forgot it.

## The Goal Machine

In 2009, Real Madrid paid a world-record transfer fee to bring Cristiano to Spain. Over the next nine years, he did something that sounds almost impossible: he scored **450 goals in 438 games**. That's more than one goal per game, for nine years!

With Real Madrid, he won the Champions League four times, including three in a row from 2016 to 2018. He became the all-time top scorer in Champions League history. He won four more Ballon d'Or awards, for a total of five.

But the moment that maybe shows his never-give-up spirit best didn't happen while he was scoring goals.

## Coaching from the Sideline

It was July 10, 2016, the final of the European Championship in Paris. Portugal were playing France, the home team, in front of a huge French crowd.

About eight minutes into the game, a French player crashed into Cristiano's knee. He went down in pain. He got up and tried to keep playing, but his knee wouldn't hold him. He went down again. Tears were streaming down his face. A moth even landed on his cheek while he was sitting on the grass. Finally, about twenty-five minutes into the biggest game of his life, he was carried off on a stretcher.

Most people thought Portugal were finished without their captain.

But Cristiano didn't go and sit in the locker room feeling sorry for himself. He got his knee strapped up, came back out, and stood right on the edge of the field next to his coach. He shouted instructions. He waved his arms. He cheered every tackle and every pass. He became a second coach, pushing his teammates on.

The game went to extra time. And then a substitute named Éder hit a long-range shot that flew into the bottom corner. **Portugal 1, France 0.**

Portugal had won their first-ever major trophy, and Cristiano, limping on one good leg, lifted it high. He had helped his team win without even being on the field. Sometimes being a leader means lifting everyone else up.

## The Man Who Never Stops

Most soccer players retire in their mid-thirties. Cristiano Ronaldo kept going, and going, and going.

He played for Juventus in Italy, then returned to Manchester United, then moved to Al Nassr in Saudi Arabia. In 2024, he became the first man to score 900 official goals in his career. In 2025, at forty years old, he scored the equalizer against Spain in the final of the UEFA Nations League, and Portugal won the trophy on penalties. Once again, he cried on the field.

At the 2026 World Cup, aged forty-one, he played at his sixth World Cup (a record he shares with Lionel Messi) and scored three goals. That made him the first player in history to score at **six different World Cups**. Portugal were knocked out by Spain in the round of 16, and Cristiano said it would be his last World Cup. But by then, he had already scored more international goals than any man in history, and more than 950 official goals in his whole career, with his eyes on the magic number of 1,000.

How does he keep going? Cristiano treats his body like a race car. He sleeps carefully, eats healthy food, does recovery exercises, stretches, and trains even when he doesn't have to. He doesn't have any tattoos, partly because he likes to donate blood, and in many places people have to wait months after getting a tattoo before they can give blood.

That crying boy from Madeira who hated to lose never really changed. He just learned how to turn his tears into fuel.

## Teamwork Spotlight: "You're Going to Score the Winner"

Remember Éder, the substitute who scored the winning goal in the Euro 2016 final? His story is a teamwork story, too.

Éder was not a superstar. Many Portuguese fans didn't even think he should be on the team. He had barely played during the tournament. But in the final, with Cristiano injured and the game stuck at 0–0, the coach sent Éder on.

Éder later told reporters that before he went onto the field, Cristiano came over to him and said something he never forgot: that he was going to score the winning goal. Cristiano believed in him when lots of other people didn't.

Éder went out, held off a French defender, and hit the shot of his life from long range. Goal!

Cristiano is famous for scoring goals himself. But that night, his biggest contribution was making a teammate feel ten feet tall. Great leaders don't only shine. They help others shine, too.

And behind Cristiano there has always been one more teammate: his mother, Dolores. She supported him when he moved away at twelve, worried about him when he had heart surgery, and has cheered for him at his biggest games. He often says she is one of the most important people in his life.

## Never-Give-Up Lesson

**Hard work beats talent when talent doesn't work hard.**

Cristiano Ronaldo was talented, but lots of kids are talented. What made him different was that he worked harder than everyone else, every single day, for more than twenty years. When people booed him, he trained more. When he got hurt in the biggest final of his life, he found another way to help his team.

Your feelings, even the frustrated, crying ones, show you how much you care. Use them as fuel!

## Fun Facts

- When Cristiano arrived at Manchester United, he actually asked for the number 28, the number he wore at Sporting. Sir Alex Ferguson gave him number 7 instead.
- His famous goal celebration ends with him landing and shouting "Siuuu!" It comes from *sí*, the Spanish word for "yes," stretched out long. Fans all over the world copy it.
- He was the first player to win the Premier League, La Liga, and Serie A titles (the top leagues in England, Spain, and Italy).
- He has scored over 140 goals for Portugal, the most by any man for his national team.
- His son, Cristiano Jr., has also played in youth soccer teams and for Portugal's youth sides.
