# Chapter 6: Ousmane Dembélé, The Comeback Kid

> **FACT FILE**
>
> **Full name:** Masour Ousmane Dembélé
>
> **Born:** May 15, 1997, in Vernon, France
>
> **Position:** Forward / winger
>
> **Famous for:** Winning the 2025 Ballon d'Or, leading Paris Saint-Germain to their first Champions League, and being able to shoot equally well with both feet
>
> **Superpower:** Getting back up, again and again and again

## Which Foot Is Your Best Foot?

Here's a question for you: are you right-footed or left-footed?

Most people know the answer straight away. Most soccer players have a "strong foot" that they use for shooting, passing, and crossing, and a "weak foot" that they use only when they have to.

Ousmane Dembélé is different. If you ask him which is his strong foot, he'll shrug and say he doesn't really know. He takes some penalties with his right foot and some corners with his left. He can dribble past a defender to the left or the right and shoot with whichever foot is closer to the ball. Defenders hate playing against him, because they can't guess which way he's going to go.

How did he get like that? Practice. As a boy growing up in Normandy, in the north of France, Ousmane spent hours kicking the ball with *both* feet, until neither one felt weaker than the other.

That kind of patience and hard work would come in very handy. Because Ousmane Dembélé's career would turn out to be one of the bumpiest roads in soccer.

## A Kid from Évreux

Ousmane was born in Vernon, a town in Normandy, and grew up nearby in Évreux. His mother's family has roots in Mauritania and Senegal, and his father's family comes from Mali. He grew up in a neighborhood called La Madeleine, where the kids played soccer on concrete courts until it got dark.

Ousmane was small, skinny, and incredibly quick. He loved to dribble. He played for local clubs in Évreux, and when he was thirteen, scouts from Stade Rennais, a big club in the region of Brittany, invited him to join their academy.

At Rennes, he kept growing as a player. At eighteen, he made his debut for the first team, and in his very first season in France's top league, Ligue 1, he scored twelve goals. People started to call him one of the most exciting young players in Europe.

## Going Up, Up, Up

In 2016, Ousmane moved to Germany to play for Borussia Dortmund, a club famous for its loud, yellow-shirted fans. He was brilliant there. He dribbled past defenders like they weren't there and set up goal after goal. At the end of his first season, he scored in the German Cup final, and Dortmund won.

Then, in the summer of 2017, came the call that changed his life.

FC Barcelona had just lost one of their biggest stars, the Brazilian Neymar, who left for Paris Saint-Germain. Barcelona needed a replacement. They chose twenty-year-old Ousmane Dembélé, and they paid a gigantic amount of money for him: at the time, it was one of the biggest transfer fees ever paid for any player.

He would be playing alongside Lionel Messi (Chapter 1!). It was a dream come true.

And then, almost right away, the dream turned into a nightmare.

## Injured, Injured, Injured

In just his third game for Barcelona, in September 2017, Ousmane tore a muscle in his leg, a hamstring injury. He was out for months.

When he came back, he got hurt again. And again. And again.

Over the next few years at Barcelona, Ousmane suffered injury after injury: muscles, ankles, knees. He missed more than a hundred games. Every time fans got excited about him, he seemed to get hurt.

People started saying mean things. Some reporters called him one of the worst signings in the history of the club. They said Barcelona had wasted their money. They said he wasn't professional enough, that he ate too much junk food, stayed up too late playing video games, and sometimes arrived late to training.

Some of that criticism was unfair. But Ousmane later admitted that some of it was true. When he was young, he didn't always take care of his body the way a top athlete needs to.

So he made a choice. Instead of getting angry at his critics, he decided to change.

## The Big Change

Ousmane started treating his body like a professional. He hired a personal chef to cook healthy meals. He worked with a nutritionist to learn what to eat. He started sleeping better, stretching more, and doing extra work with physiotherapists to make his muscles stronger.

It didn't fix everything overnight. He still got some injuries. But slowly, he started playing more and more games in a row. And when he played, he was magical.

He also helped France win the 2018 World Cup in Russia, alongside Kylian Mbappé (Chapter 5!). And he won three Spanish league titles with Barcelona. But still, many people thought of him as the player who could have been great, if only he weren't always injured.

There were more painful moments, too. In the 2022 World Cup final against Argentina, Ousmane started the game, but France were struggling so badly that their coach took him off before halftime. It was a hard thing to go through on the biggest stage in the world. Ousmane didn't complain in public. He just kept working.

In 2023, he left Barcelona and moved back home to France to play for Paris Saint-Germain.

## Dropped by the Coach

At PSG, Ousmane's coach was a tough Spanish manager named Luis Enrique. Luis Enrique demanded a lot from his players. He wanted them to run hard, press hard, and defend as a team.

In the autumn of 2024, Luis Enrique left Ousmane out of the squad for a big Champions League game against Arsenal. He said that the player hadn't met the standards the team needed.

Being left out like that, in front of the whole world, was embarrassing. Some people thought Ousmane might sulk, or even ask to leave.

He didn't. He went back to training and worked harder. He started pressing defenders like a man possessed. He tracked back. He did the hard, unglamorous running that wins games.

And then Luis Enrique tried something new.

## The New Number 9

For most of his career, Ousmane had been a winger, a player who runs down the side of the field. But in late 2024, Luis Enrique moved him into the middle, to play as a center forward.

It was like flipping a switch.

Ousmane started scoring. And scoring. And scoring. From the middle of the field, with his two great feet, he could shoot from anywhere. In that 2024–25 season, he scored thirty-five goals in all competitions.

PSG won the French league. They won the French Cup. And then they went on an incredible run in the Champions League, a trophy that PSG, for all their famous players, had never won.

On May 31, 2025, in Munich, PSG played Inter Milan in the final. PSG won **5–0**, the biggest winning margin in any Champions League final ever. Ousmane set up two goals. Paris Saint-Germain were champions of Europe for the first time.

And Ousmane, the player once called a waste of money, was at the heart of it all.

## Tears at the Ballon d'Or

On September 22, 2025, the biggest names in soccer gathered in a theater in Paris for the Ballon d'Or ceremony. The nominees included Lamine Yamal (Chapter 12!), Kylian Mbappé, Mohamed Salah, and many others.

The award was presented by the Brazilian legend Ronaldinho. He opened the envelope and read out the name:

**Ousmane Dembélé.**

Ousmane walked up to the stage with tears in his eyes. He held the golden ball and, in his speech, he thanked the people who had helped him through all the hard times: his coaches, his teammates, and especially his mother, who was sitting in the audience crying, too.

A few months later, he also won FIFA's award for The Best Men's Player of 2025.

## Still Fighting

Ousmane's story didn't become perfectly smooth after that. Early in the 2025–26 season, he got injured again. But this time, he knew exactly what to do. He worked his way back, and once again, he helped PSG all the way to the Champions League final.

On May 30, 2026, in Budapest, PSG faced Arsenal (whose star winger, Bukayo Saka, you'll meet in Chapter 9). The final ended 1–1, and PSG won the penalty shootout to become champions of Europe for the **second year in a row**.

The kid who had been injured more than almost anyone, and criticized more than almost anyone, had become one of the best players on the planet.

## Teamwork Spotlight: Defending from the Front

When PSG won the Champions League in 2025, they didn't have one superstar doing everything. They had a team.

Luis Enrique asked every player to work hard without the ball. That meant that when PSG lost possession, the forwards didn't stand around waiting. They sprinted to win it back. Ousmane, the center forward, became the first defender, chasing goalkeepers and center backs and forcing them into mistakes.

Around him, young players like Désiré Doué and Khvicha Kvaratskhelia ran just as hard. The midfielders, Vitinha, João Neves, and Fabián Ruiz, passed and pressed like a machine. Achraf Hakimi and Nuno Mendes flew up and down the sides of the field.

Luis Enrique said Ousmane deserved the Ballon d'Or not only because of his goals, but because of how hard he worked to defend for his team. That's a big change from the player who once got criticized for not being professional!

The lesson? You don't have to be the star of every moment. Sometimes the most important thing you can do is the hard, tiring job that helps your teammates, like sprinting back to win the ball. When everyone does that, a team becomes almost impossible to beat.

## Never-Give-Up Lesson

**Listen to honest feedback, and be willing to change.**

When people criticized Ousmane, some of what they said was unfair. But some of it was true. The most powerful thing he did was admit that he needed to change his habits, and then actually do it. Getting dropped by his coach didn't make him quit. It made him work harder.

Sometimes the toughest coach, or the toughest feedback, is exactly what helps you grow.

## Fun Facts

- Ousmane's nickname in France is sometimes "Dembouz."
- He is one of very few top players who is truly "two-footed," able to shoot, cross, and dribble just as well with either foot.
- PSG's 5–0 win over Inter in the 2025 Champions League final is the biggest winning score in the history of the final.
- He won the World Cup with France in 2018 and the Ballon d'Or in 2025, seven years later.
- In 2025 he swept the two biggest individual prizes in soccer, the Ballon d'Or and FIFA's The Best award, in the same year that his club won the Champions League for the first time.
