# Final Whistle: Your Story Starts Now {.unnumbered}

You've just met twelve of the greatest soccer players on the planet.

You've read about a boy who needed injections every night just to grow. A boy who moved away from his family at twelve. A refugee who played soccer in a hotel parking lot while war went on around him. A teenager who rode up to five buses a day to get to practice. A kid who covered his bedroom walls with posters of his hero, and then grew up to play for that hero's club. A player who got injured so many times that people called him a waste of money, until he became the best in the world. A dancer who refused to stop dancing. A striker who couldn't score in his first sixteen games. A nineteen-year-old who missed a penalty in front of the whole world and refused to let it break him. A boy who was always the youngest on the field. A young man who left the only club he'd ever known because he believed in himself. And a kid from Rocafonda who was given a bath by Lionel Messi and grew up to beat him in a World Cup final.

Did you notice what all their stories have in common?

**Every one of them had a moment when it would have been easier to quit.**

Messi actually *did* quit the national team, for a few weeks, before he changed his mind. Ronaldo could have gone home to Madeira. Modrić could have believed Hajduk Split when they said he was too small. Salah could have stayed on the bench at Chelsea. Mbappé could have walked away from France's team. Dembélé could have sulked when his coach dropped him. Vinícius could have stopped dancing, or stopped speaking up. Haaland could have decided he wasn't a real striker. Saka could have never taken another penalty. Bellingham could have stopped believing in the last minutes. Yıldız could have believed Bayern were right about him. And Yamal could have let the losses and the injuries and the pressure take away his joy.

None of them did.

Now, here's the most important thing in this whole book: **you don't have to become a famous soccer player for these lessons to matter.**

Maybe your dream is to make the school team. Maybe it's to become a doctor, a teacher, a scientist, an artist, a coach, or something nobody has even invented yet. Maybe right now your biggest challenge is a hard math test, a new school, a friend who's being unkind, or a skill that just won't click no matter how much you practice.

The same rules apply.

- When someone says you're too small, too slow, or not good enough, remember Luka and Leo.
- When you make a big mistake in front of everyone, remember Bukayo and Kylian.
- When you're far from home or feeling lonely, remember Cristiano and Mo.
- When you're getting criticized, remember Ousmane, who listened, changed, and became the best.
- When you're treated unfairly, remember Vini, who stood up and kept his joy.
- When you're starting slowly, remember Erling's sixteen games without a goal.
- When it looks like it's all over, remember Jude's bicycle kick in the 95th minute.
- When people don't believe in you, remember Kenan, who believed in himself.
- And when you start to succeed, remember Lamine and the "304," and never forget where you came from.

Every hero in this book started out as a kid with a ball and a dream. Just like you.

So lace up your boots. Grab your ball. Open your books. Go chase your dream.

And whatever happens: **never give up.**

# The Never-Give-Up Playbook {.unnumbered}

Coaches use a playbook to plan their best moves. Here is your playbook: twelve big lessons from twelve heroes, and one thing you can try for each.

**1. Your weakness can become your superpower. (Lionel Messi)**
Leo's small size made him quick and hard to knock off the ball.
*Try this:* Write down one thing you think is a weakness. Then write one way it might actually help you.

**2. Hard work beats talent when talent doesn't work hard. (Cristiano Ronaldo)**
Cristiano outworked everyone for more than twenty years.
*Try this:* Pick one skill and practice it for ten extra minutes every day this week.

**3. Hard times can make you stronger. (Luka Modrić)**
War, rejection, and being called the "worst signing" made Luka tougher.
*Try this:* Think of a hard time you've already gotten through. What did it teach you?

**4. Go the extra mile, and when one door closes, find another. (Mohamed Salah)**
Mo rode buses for hours, and when Chelsea didn't work out, he found success in Italy.
*Try this:* If something isn't working, brainstorm three different ways to reach the same goal.

**5. A miss doesn't define you. What you do next does. (Kylian Mbappé)**
Kylian missed a big penalty, then stepped up again in a World Cup final.
*Try this:* The next time you make a mistake, say to yourself, "That's one try. What's my next try?"

**6. Listen to honest feedback, and be willing to change. (Ousmane Dembélé)**
Ousmane changed his habits and became the best player in the world.
*Try this:* Ask a coach, teacher, or parent for one thing you could do better. Then work on it.

**7. Stand up for what's right, and protect your joy. (Vinícius Júnior)**
Vini spoke out against racism and never stopped dancing.
*Try this:* If you see someone being treated unfairly, stand next to them or tell a trusted adult.

**8. It doesn't matter how fast you start. (Erling Haaland)**
Erling went sixteen games without a goal, then broke scoring records.
*Try this:* Keep a "progress log." Write down how you're doing at something today, then check again in a month.

**9. Your hardest moment doesn't decide who you are. (Bukayo Saka)**
Bukayo bounced back from a painful miss with courage and kindness.
*Try this:* Send a kind message to someone who's having a tough time.

**10. It's never over until the final whistle. (Jude Bellingham)**
Jude scored in the 95th minute when England were about to go home.
*Try this:* Finish everything you start, even when you're tired: the last lap, the last question, the last page.

**11. Other people's doubts don't have to become yours. (Kenan Yıldız)**
Kenan believed in himself when his old club didn't.
*Try this:* Write a "belief note" to yourself: three things you are good at and one big goal. Read it when you feel doubted.

**12. Stay humble and remember where you came from. (Lamine Yamal)**
Lamine still makes the "304" sign for his neighborhood.
*Try this:* Thank someone who has helped you on your journey: a parent, grandparent, coach, or teacher.

# Soccer Words to Know {.unnumbered}

**Academy:** A club's school for young players, where they train and learn to become professionals. Barcelona's is called La Masia; Arsenal's is called Hale End.

**Assist:** The pass that sets up a goal for a teammate.

**Ballon d'Or:** French for "Golden Ball." It's a famous yearly award for the best soccer player in the world.

**Bench:** Where substitutes sit during a game, waiting for a chance to play.

**Bicycle kick:** An acrobatic kick where a player jumps, throws their legs over their head, and kicks the ball backward over themselves.

**Captain:** The player who leads the team on the field and wears an armband.

**Champions League:** The biggest competition for club teams in Europe.

**Copa América:** The biggest competition for national teams in South America.

**Cutback:** A pass played backward from near the goal line into the penalty area, usually for a teammate to shoot.

**Debut:** A player's very first game for a team.

**Dribble:** To run with the ball while keeping it close to your feet, often to get past defenders.

**Euros (European Championship):** The biggest competition for national teams in Europe, held every four years.

**Extra time:** Two extra periods of fifteen minutes each, played in knockout games when the score is tied after ninety minutes.

**Free agent:** A player whose contract has ended, so they can join a new club without a transfer fee.

**Futsal:** A fast, five-a-side version of soccer played indoors on a hard court with a smaller, heavier ball.

**Golden Ball:** The award for the best player at a World Cup.

**Golden Boot:** The award for the top goal scorer in a league or tournament.

**Hat-trick:** When one player scores three goals in a single game.

**Knockout stage:** The part of a tournament where the losing team is eliminated, and only the winner moves on.

**La Liga:** The top professional league in Spain.

**Ligue 1:** The top professional league in France.

**Loan:** When a club lets one of its players play for another club for a period of time.

**Midfielder:** A player who plays in the middle of the field, helping both to defend and attack.

**Nations League:** A competition for European national teams, with a final tournament.

**Non-league:** Soccer played below the top professional divisions, often by part-time players.

**Penalty kick:** A free shot at goal from the penalty spot, given when a defender fouls an attacker inside the penalty area.

**Penalty shootout:** A way of deciding a tied knockout game: players take turns shooting penalties, usually five per team, and the team that scores more wins.

**Premier League:** The top professional league in England.

**Pressing:** When players chase and close down opponents to win the ball back quickly.

**Refugee:** A person forced to leave their home or country because of war, danger, or disaster.

**Serie A:** The top professional league in Italy.

**Stoppage time:** Extra minutes added at the end of each half to make up for time lost during the game (for injuries, substitutions, and so on).

**Striker:** An attacking player whose main job is to score goals.

**Transfer:** When a player moves from one club to another, often for a fee.

**Treble:** Winning three major trophies in one season, usually the league, the national cup, and the Champions League.

**Trivela:** A pass or shot struck with the outside of the foot, making the ball curve.

**Winger:** An attacking player who plays near the sidelines and tries to dribble past defenders, cross, or cut inside to shoot.

**World Cup:** The biggest soccer tournament on Earth, played by national teams every four years.

# For Parents, Teachers, and Coaches {.unnumbered}

Thank you for sharing this book with a young reader. Each chapter is written to be read in about fifteen to twenty minutes, which makes it ideal for bedtime reading, a reading group, or a classroom read-aloud.

The stories touch on some serious topics in an age-appropriate way: war and refugees (Chapter 3), the loss of family members (Chapters 1, 2, and 3), a stadium tragedy (Chapter 4), and racism and online abuse (Chapters 5, 7, and 9). You may want to read these chapters together and pause for conversation. Here are some questions that work well with any chapter:

1. What was the biggest obstacle this player faced? Was it something they could control, or not?
2. Who helped this player along the way? Who helps *you*?
3. What choice did the player make that changed their story?
4. Have you ever felt the way this player felt? What did you do?
5. What is one thing from this chapter you could try this week?

**Ideas for the classroom or team:**

- **Hero Report:** Each child chooses a player and presents a one-minute "Fact File" of their own, then adds one new fact they discovered.
- **Comeback Wall:** Make a bulletin board where kids post (anonymously, if they like) a time they didn't give up.
- **Kindness Penalty:** Inspired by the support Bukayo Saka received, have the group write encouraging notes to someone who is going through a tough time.
- **Map the Heroes:** On a world map, mark where each hero was born and every club they played for. Talk about how far each one traveled.
- **Practice Plan:** Using the Weekly Practice Tracker in this book, have kids set and track a small goal for one week, in soccer or in anything else.

The core message of this book is that success is not about never failing. It is about what we do *after* we fail. Talent opens doors, but courage, teamwork, and persistence are what carry people through them.

# A Note on Facts and Sources {.unnumbered}

Everything in this book is based on real events, drawn from news reports, interviews, match records, and club and tournament information available up to October 2026. Soccer statistics change quickly, so numbers like goals, games, and trophies may be different by the time you read this.

To help young readers feel what these moments were like, some scenes describe what a player might have been thinking or feeling, and a few short bits of everyday conversation (like a coach's question to a young player) are retold in our own words. Those details are imagined, but the events themselves really happened. Where a player's public statement is quoted directly, it is printed in italics with quotation marks.

This book is not affiliated with, or endorsed by, any of the players, clubs, leagues, or organizations mentioned. We simply admire them, and we hope their stories inspire you.
